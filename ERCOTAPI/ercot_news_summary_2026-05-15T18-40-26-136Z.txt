- Chevron is reportedly seeking a large Texas tax break to build a dedicated power plant for a data center, underscoring how large-load growth is shifting from standard ERCOT interconnection toward behind-the-meter or co-located supply models that could bypass shared grid constraints but still affect fuel, transmission, and reliability planning if backup or export arrangements touch the ERCOT system. (Mother Jones)

- Local resistance to new data center and storage projects is becoming a material Texas grid-development risk: Hill County approved a one-year moratorium, and a major North Texas data center project drew strong opposition, signaling that siting, water use, noise, and land-use conflicts may slow load additions, storage builds, and associated transmission upgrades even where ERCOT demand growth is otherwise accelerating. (Insurance Journal; Dallas Observer)

- ERCOT’s demand outlook keeps tilting toward data-center-driven load growth, with new South Texas modular AI data center expansion announcements and broader national coverage of data center mapping and rural buildouts reinforcing that planners should expect more clustered, transmission-sensitive large loads with potentially fast timelines and significant operational ramping needs. (PRNewswire; The Verge)

- EIA now forecasts utility-scale solar generation will exceed coal generation in ERCOT in 2026 for the first time, highlighting a structural shift toward more energy from weather-dependent resources and deeper reliance on storage, flexible thermal fleet performance, ancillary services, and transmission to manage net-load variability and maintain reliability margins. (EIA)

- Market and infrastructure vendors are increasingly targeting the data-center power bottleneck with technologies such as solid-state transformers, lower-energy optical switching, and advanced power semiconductors; while mostly early-stage, the theme is relevant for ERCOT because any reduction in conversion losses, interconnection lead times, or on-site power-quality constraints could modestly ease large-load integration pressure over time. (PRNewswire; PRNewswire; PRNewswire)

- The broader policy backdrop is turning more contentious for hyperscale load growth: multiple outlets report rising public opposition to AI data centers nationwide over power and water use, which matters for ERCOT because community and political pushback can translate into permitting friction, local moratoria, and tougher scrutiny of whether new generation and transmission are serving public reliability needs or dedicated private loads. (Forbes; Fortune; The Verge)

- Supply-chain and cyber risk remain relevant to ERCOT infrastructure expansion, as Foxconn confirmed a cyberattack affecting North American operations; while not ERCOT-specific, disruptions at major electronics manufacturers can ripple into transformer, controls, server, and industrial equipment availability, potentially affecting data center build schedules and power-system upgrade timelines. (MacDailyNews; The Times of India)

- New financing and industrial development signals around power-intensive growth in Texas—including Permian digital infrastructure positioning and a state grant supporting the Round Top rare-earth project—reinforce the state’s broader trend toward electricity-intensive industrial expansion, which can tighten ERCOT planning needs for generation adequacy, transmission expansion, and localized delivery capability beyond just traditional oil-and-gas load centers. (GlobeNewswire; GlobeNewswire)
