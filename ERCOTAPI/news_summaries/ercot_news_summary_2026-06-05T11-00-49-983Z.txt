- B2U and Waymo announced a battery second-life partnership that would route retired autonomous EV batteries into stationary storage projects in Texas, pointing to an emerging supply channel for ERCOT battery installations beyond new-cell manufacturing. If deployed at scale, repurposed batteries could add flexible capacity for peak support and ancillary services, though project economics, performance degradation, and interconnection timing will matter. (GlobeNewswire; Electrek)
- The Waymo/B2U deal is also a planning signal: ERCOT may see more nontraditional BESS development models tied to EV fleets and circular supply chains, which could modestly ease battery sourcing constraints while expanding distributed or front-of-meter storage options in high-growth load areas. (GlobeNewswire; Electrek)
- Multiple reports highlight intensifying backlash to AI/data center expansion over electricity costs, water use, and local transparency, including Texas-specific coverage and references to West Texas projects. For ERCOT, the key takeaway is that large-load growth is no longer just an interconnection issue; public acceptance, water constraints, and cost-allocation debates are becoming material risks to siting, timing, and transmission planning. (Forbes; DW; Wired; 9to5Google; Commercial Observer; Columbia Climate Blog)
- Google’s new water replenishment commitments and Texas water fund underscore that data center developers are increasingly trying to address local environmental opposition before it slows projects. In ERCOT, that matters because water availability and community pushback can affect where hyperscale load actually materializes versus where it is proposed, complicating forecast accuracy for generation and transmission buildout. (9to5Google; Wired; Slashdot)
- Broader data center investment stories continue to reinforce the scale of prospective U.S. load growth, with Texas and Austin repeatedly cited in market outlooks and project coverage. For ERCOT, the implication is sustained pressure on large-load interconnection processes, substation and transmission upgrades, and scrutiny of whether new load brings enough dispatchable supply, storage, or demand flexibility to avoid worsening reliability and congestion. (PRNewswire; GlobeNewswire; CNBC; Yahoo Finance)
