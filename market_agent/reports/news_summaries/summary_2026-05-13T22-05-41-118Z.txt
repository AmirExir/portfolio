- **Walmart trims corporate roles:** The retailer will lay off or relocate about **1,000 employees** as it consolidates tech and product teams—another sign big companies are still prioritizing efficiency over headcount growth.

- **Inflation re-accelerates:** U.S. consumer prices rose **3.8%** year over year, with the Iran war driving gasoline costs higher. The takeaway: energy is back as a macro risk, complicating any path to lower interest rates.

- **Oil market stress is intensifying:** Reports point to rapidly shrinking crude stockpiles and shipping disruptions through key routes. If sustained, this could worsen inflation and hit transport-heavy sectors.

- **Tariff refunds start flowing:** Following a Supreme Court ruling against some Trump-era tariffs, companies are beginning to receive refunds. That offers a modest cash boost for affected importers and manufacturers.

- **Fed politics get tougher:** Kevin Warsh’s arrival at the Fed may not translate into quick rate cuts, as inflation pressure and internal divisions limit flexibility despite political pressure from Trump.

- **Amazon pushes speed again:** Amazon is expanding **30-minute delivery hubs**, signaling a new phase of competition in convenience retail. Great for consumers, but likely expensive and margin-sensitive.

- **AI scrutiny keeps rising:** Sam Altman testified about an unsettling early safety discussion with Elon Musk, while separate reports say Altman’s business dealings are drawing GOP scrutiny ahead of a possible OpenAI IPO. AI’s commercial future is increasingly tied to governance and trust.

- **AI liability risk deepens:** A lawsuit alleges ChatGPT gave dangerous drug advice to a teen who later died. This could become a major legal and regulatory test case for consumer-facing AI tools.

- **Big Tech’s AI ambitions are getting more extreme:** Google and SpaceX are reportedly discussing **orbital data centers**, underscoring how far companies may go to secure future AI computing capacity.

- **Labor costs are still climbing:** United Airlines flight attendants approved a deal with **31% raises**, highlighting continued wage pressure in unionized sectors even as some white-collar layoffs mount.

- **GM layoffs underscore automation anxiety:** Newly laid-off employees described abrupt job cuts, with AI reportedly part of the backdrop. It reinforces the split economy: productivity upgrades for firms, insecurity for workers.

- **Nvidia’s China challenge remains political:** CEO Jensen Huang wants access to China, but geopolitical priorities may override business interests. For chipmakers, policy risk remains as important as product demand.

- **Market bubble warnings are back:** Michael Burry says current conditions resemble the late stages of the dot-com era. Whether or not he’s right, stretched AI-driven valuations are clearly becoming a bigger debate on Wall Street.
