- Texas political and local opposition to data-center growth is intensifying, with Gov. Abbott calling for self-supplied power in some cases, San Marcos adopting zoning restrictions, and communities pushing back on rural siting. For ERCOT, that raises the odds of more behind-the-meter generation proposals, contested interconnection queues, and slower realization of large-load demand forecasts used in planning. (Dallas Observer; TechRadar)

- A proposed $25 billion AI data-center campus near Abilene tied to OpenAI/Oracle/Vantage would be one of the larger prospective new ERCOT loads if built, with implications for West Texas transmission, deliverability, and timing risk between load additions and supporting infrastructure. The key planning issue is whether load materializes on schedule or lags, which directly affects ERCOT’s capacity and congestion outlook. (Crypto Briefing)

- Texas data centers are drawing broader scrutiny beyond power demand, including water-use reporting gaps and environmental constraints. For ERCOT, that matters because cooling-water limitations and local permitting friction can alter project design, siting, and in-service dates, making large-load interconnection forecasts less certain. (Natural News; Dallas Observer)

- Oilfield services wages hit another record as higher crude prices spur drilling, a signal that Texas upstream activity may keep industrial load, gas supply infrastructure use, and generation fuel availability tight. In ERCOT terms, stronger drilling economics can support gas production over time but also reinforce labor and equipment cost pressure for new thermal builds and maintenance. (Financial Post/Bloomberg)

- Multiple national stories continue to reinforce the same ERCOT-relevant theme: AI and high-performance computing loads are driving interest in dedicated gas generation, microgrids, miner-to-data-center conversions, and other off-grid or hybrid power solutions. That trend could reduce some direct ERCOT load growth, but it also complicates visibility into true system demand, resource adequacy, and transmission planning assumptions. (Cointelegraph; Reuters via Times of India; PR Newswire)

- Broader power-sector commentary points to a softer near-term reliability outlook only because some large loads are connecting more slowly than expected. For ERCOT, that is an important caution: any delay in data-center energization can temporarily ease reserve concerns, but it does not eliminate the longer-term need for generation, transmission, and interconnection reforms if those loads eventually arrive. (POWER magazine)
