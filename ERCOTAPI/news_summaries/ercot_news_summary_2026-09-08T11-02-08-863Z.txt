- ERCOT posted a cluster of rule changes aimed at tightening how reliability products are offered and priced: NPRR1347 would set Energy Offer Curves for procured Contract for Capacity resources at the effective Value of Lost Load, sharpening scarcity-performance incentives and likely affecting offer behavior during tight conditions. (ERCOT NPRR1347)
- Fuel and emergency cost recovery rules are also moving: NPRR1346 would double the fuel-oil transportation adder in the Fuel Oil Price from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, while NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost process sunset from 2027 to 2030, signaling ERCOT expects fuel-cost volatility and outage-response cost issues to remain relevant in reliability operations. (ERCOT NPRR1346; ERCOT NPRR1345)
- ERCOT is proposing a Day-Ahead Market conduct change through NPRR1343, adding a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids; that should discourage low-value congestion positioning, improve DAM efficiency, and reduce distortions in transmission congestion pricing. (ERCOT NPRR1343)
- Governance and testing oversight are being formalized: NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, giving market-test procedures a more standardized approval path and reducing ambiguity around implementation governance for market changes. (ERCOT NPRR1344)
- Large load interconnection remains a major planning focus: PGRR145 would create a “Batch Zero” process to study system-wide reliability impacts of mature large-load projects, while PGRR144 would tighten dynamic model submission requirements for large loads including Large Computational Loads. Together, these changes point to ERCOT building a more rigorous screening framework for data centers and other high-impact loads before they drive transmission or stability issues. (ERCOT PGRR145; ERCOT PGRR144)
- ERCOT is also easing one near-term hurdle for large computational loads: NOGRR289 would allow certain LCL cooling loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet ride-through requirements, offering commissioning flexibility but also highlighting ERCOT’s concern that these new load types must ultimately perform through disturbances without worsening reliability events. (ERCOT NOGRR289)
- Storage and controllable demand are being pulled deeper into reliability and market design: NPRR1182 would include Controllable Load Resources and Energy Storage Resources in the Constraint Competitiveness Test process, and NOGRR288 would add ESR participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. That expands ERCOT’s toolkit for congestion management and reserves, while increasing the operational and compliance role of batteries and flexible load. (ERCOT NPRR1182; ERCOT NOGRR288)
- Transmission planning and bulk-system operations updates continue to reflect a larger, more complex grid: NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits, and PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for Category P6/N-1-1 contingencies. These are foundational changes for operating and planning a higher-voltage, more heavily stressed ERCOT system. (ERCOT NOGRR286; ERCOT PGRR147)
- Load-shed and critical-load governance are being refined through NOGRR287, which would have TOs and TDSPs collaborate to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads in firm load shed plans—an operationally important step to make emergency shedding more targeted and effective during extreme grid events. (ERCOT NOGRR287)
- Generator interconnection transparency is also advancing: PGRR131 would require interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators, and PGRR132 clarifies new resources must interconnect via a new SGIA. The likely impact is clearer cost visibility and less ambiguity for developers evaluating queue economics and project timelines. (ERCOT PGRR131; ERCOT PGRR132)
- ERCOT posted an update to OBDRR055 on Non-Spinning Reserve deployment and recall procedures for RTC+B alignment. Even without full detail, the title indicates ERCOT is refining how Non-Spin is actually deployed and recalled under evolving real-time commitment tools—important for reserve performance, operator actions, and battery/thermal participation rules. (ERCOT OBDRR055)
