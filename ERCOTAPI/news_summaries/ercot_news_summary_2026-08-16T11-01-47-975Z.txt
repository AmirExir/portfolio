- ERCOT posted a major large-load planning package centered on PGRR145, which would create a transitional “Batch Zero” process to study high-maturity large load interconnections on a system-wide basis rather than one-off; this is a direct response to accelerating demand from large computational loads and other big new entrants. Grid impact: it should improve visibility into clustered reliability and transmission effects before energization. Planning/governance angle: it signals ERCOT is moving toward more centralized queue discipline and study prioritization for large loads. (PGRR145, ERCOT)

- ERCOT also advanced companion large-load modeling reforms under PGRR144, requiring clearer dynamic model submission and review for large loads including large computational loads; that matters because transient and voltage behavior from these facilities is now material to stability studies, not just peak demand forecasts. (PGRR144, ERCOT)

- New stakeholder filings were posted for PGRR145, including LCRA comments and PUCT Staff comments; even without full comment detail, the filings indicate active debate over how Batch Zero should screen projects, allocate study priority, and balance customer development timelines against reliability risk from speculative load requests. (145PGRR-57 LCRA Comments 042726; 145PGRR-58 PUCT Staff Comments 042926, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to give Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet certain cooling-load ride-through requirements where needed; what happened is a proposed compliance extension, but the operational implication is that ERCOT is trying to accommodate fast-moving LCL development while still preserving post-disturbance performance expectations. Reliability angle: this may speed energization, but it also highlights concern that large new loads can materially affect disturbance performance if protection and cooling systems are not fully aligned at COD. (NOGRR289, ERCOT)

- ERCOT proposed NOGRR286 to add 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important marker that extra-high-voltage infrastructure is becoming operationally relevant in ERCOT planning and procedures. Grid impact: clearer voltage criteria at 765 kV supports secure operation of future backbone transmission. Planning angle: this points to longer-term transmission expansion and the need to codify how higher-voltage assets are run under normal and contingency conditions. (NOGRR286, ERCOT)

- Market design updates include NPRR1343, which would charge a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market; the likely effect is to discourage low-value congestion gaming/noise and improve DAM efficiency. Market implication: cleaner congestion signals could sharpen CRR/PTP behavior and reduce uplift or inefficiencies tied to uneconomic transmission bidding. (NPRR1343, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a mitigated offer cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT’s authority; this is notable because it tightens how reliability-procured resources are priced into the market. Operational/market angle: better-defined mitigation rules can reduce over-recovery risk while preserving ERCOT’s ability to secure emergency capacity when needed. (NPRR1342, ERCOT)

- NPRR1345 would extend the sunset of the exceptional fuel cost process from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030, indicating ERCOT still sees a need for a formal path to recognize extraordinary fuel cost conditions. Reliability/market angle: retaining the mechanism may help keep thermal units available during stressed fuel environments, but it also prolongs a post-crisis market accommodation that participants will watch closely for incentive effects. (NPRR1345, ERCOT)

- ERCOT and stakeholders continue adapting reserve products for storage: NOGRR288, tied to NPRR1340, adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. Grid impact: broader ESR participation should increase fast-response reliability tools and improve reserve flexibility during tight conditions. Planning implication: it further integrates batteries into core reliability services rather than treating them as energy-only assets. (NOGRR288, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 to refine load-shed provisions so TOs and TDSPs identify and minimize overlap of essential loads; what happened is a procedural update, but the operational consequence is potentially more targeted firm load shed during emergencies. Reliability/governance angle: this is a Winter Storm Uri-era resilience refinement aimed at reducing unintended impacts to critical services while preserving ERCOT emergency control effectiveness. (NOGRR287, ERCOT)

- A governance/process thread also appeared in NPRR1344, which would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19 to standardize approval; this is less about real-time operations today and more about formalizing how ERCOT validates market-system changes before implementation, reducing ambiguity in future market releases. (NPRR1344, ERCOT)
