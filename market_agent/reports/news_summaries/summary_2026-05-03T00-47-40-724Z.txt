- **Markets:** The **S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record highs** to start May, helped by **Apple’s rally** and **cooler oil prices**. The **Dow lagged**, suggesting leadership remains concentrated in mega-cap tech.

- **Apple:** Apple issued a **stronger-than-expected revenue outlook**, easing some investor fears even as it warned about **higher memory-chip costs** and **ongoing Mac shortages**. Takeaway: demand looks resilient, but supply constraints haven’t disappeared.

- **Airlines:** **Spirit Airlines may shut down as soon as Saturday** if a **$500 million government aid package** doesn’t materialize. This is a major stress signal for the low-cost travel segment and could tighten capacity on budget routes.

- **M&A:** **GameStop is reportedly preparing an offer for eBay**, a surprising potential deal that would mark a dramatic strategic swing. If serious, it raises questions about financing, integration, and whether GameStop is trying to reinvent itself through scale.

- **AI / Big Tech:** A key theme today is that **infrastructure and compute are becoming the real competitive moat** in AI. Google’s market reaction reinforced the idea that owning the chips, data centers, and distribution may matter as much as having the best model.

- **Defense tech:** The **Pentagon signed agreements with several major AI companies** to deploy technology in **classified military networks**, signaling that defense is becoming a more meaningful commercial and strategic customer for frontier AI firms.

- **Energy:** Oil was mixed in the headlines: **spot prices cooled enough to support stocks**, but some traders are betting **U.S. crude could surge above $125** if geopolitical tensions persist. Bottom line: energy volatility remains a macro wildcard.

- **Currencies:** **Japan’s intervention boosted the yen sharply**, and markets are now watching for follow-up action. This matters globally because renewed FX intervention can ripple into bond markets, carry trades, and multinational earnings.

- **Executive pay:** Tesla disclosed **Elon Musk’s 2025 compensation value at about $158.4 billion**, underscoring the continuing scale of performance-based equity awards and the widening spotlight on CEO pay versus employee median compensation.

- **Consumer/health crossover:** An unusual trend: makers of **gum and oral-care products may benefit from side effects tied to GLP-1 weight-loss drugs**. It’s a niche story, but another example of how blockbuster health trends can reshape adjacent consumer categories.
