- **U.S. stocks look softer after Big Tech earnings:** S&P 500 futures dipped as investors parsed results from **Alphabet and Amazon**, showing that even strong earnings may not fully satisfy a market demanding clear AI payoff and margin resilience.

- **AI race is reshaping tech leadership:** Coverage suggests **Alphabet and Amazon are outpacing Meta in AI narrative and execution**, while **Microsoft** is also getting support from strong **Azure** expectations. The market is rewarding credible AI monetization, not just spending.

- **Meta’s selloff highlights higher standards:** Despite solid earnings, **Meta shares fell sharply**, a sign that investors are increasingly sensitive to valuation, guidance, and capex intensity rather than backward-looking beats.

- **Alphabet’s business mix keeps evolving:** **Google Cloud now accounts for roughly 18% of Alphabet revenue**, reinforcing that the company is becoming less dependent on search and more leveraged to enterprise/cloud growth.

- **Europe faces a stagflation-like squeeze:** **Euro zone inflation rose to 3% while growth nearly stalled**, complicating the ECB outlook and raising the risk of a weaker growth backdrop alongside sticky prices.

- **China’s factory data was better than feared, but still cooling:** Manufacturing activity topped expectations, yet **new orders softened**, suggesting demand remains fragile and any rebound may lack momentum.

- **Oil is climbing on geopolitical risk:** Crude prices surged as the **Iran standoff** shows little sign of easing, adding another inflation risk for central banks and another cost pressure for transport-heavy sectors.

- **Fed policy uncertainty remains a market overhang:** Reports around **Jerome Powell staying on the Fed board** and comments tied to **Kevin Warsh** underscore that leadership and policy direction are still moving markets, especially as rate-cut expectations stay fragile.

- **Airline sector stress is intensifying:** **Spirit Airlines bailout talks reportedly stalled**, raising collapse concerns and underscoring the financial strain in the low-cost carrier space.

- **Operational and regulatory risks are back in focus:** A **United flight reported a possible drone strike**, while transit and legal headlines—from a potential **LIRR strike** to **Musk’s courtroom scrutiny**—remind investors that non-earnings risks can quickly affect sentiment and costs.
