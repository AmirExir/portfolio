- **Oil and geopolitics back in focus:** Oil jumped roughly 3%–4% after Trump rejected Iran’s response to a ceasefire proposal, pressuring stock futures and reminding markets that Middle East risk remains a live macro driver.

- **Markets show uneven risk appetite:** Asian shares were mixed after fresh Wall Street records, suggesting investors are balancing strong equity momentum against rising energy prices and geopolitical uncertainty.

- **China signals confidence ahead of Trump-Xi talks:** Beijing fixed the yuan at a three-year high, a notable policy signal as leaders prepare to meet; separate data suggest deflation pressures may be easing, though higher input costs are building.

- **China’s factory inflation is accelerating:** Producer prices rose at the fastest pace since the pandemic, largely tied to the Iran war’s cost shock—an important sign that global supply-side inflation risks are not fully gone.

- **European oil majors are cashing in on volatility:** BP, Shell, and TotalEnergies reportedly generated up to **$4.75 billion** from trading during Iran-war turbulence, highlighting how commodity volatility is creating winners even as it raises broader economic risks.

- **AI market exuberance is intact:** Cerebras is reportedly lifting its IPO price range as demand surges, while OpenAI employees are being portrayed as holding one of the most valuable “lottery tickets” of the AI boom.

- **South Korea is gaining favor with global banks:** JPMorgan raised its bullish target on the Kospi to **10,000**, citing a stronger memory-chip cycle, governance reforms, and industrial growth—a sign AI and semiconductors remain central equity themes.

- **U.S. labor sentiment looks unusually weak:** Axios reports America has the world’s largest optimism gap on the job market, with older Americans especially pessimistic—potentially a warning sign for consumer confidence and hiring expectations.

- **Wall Street’s outlook is splitting further:** Ed Yardeni sees the S&P 500 reaching **8,250** this year on resilient earnings, while Michael Burry is invoking historical warning signs—underscoring a market torn between strong fundamentals and bubble fears.

- **Fed credibility remains under scrutiny:** Commentary around Powell’s legacy frames the central bank as still fighting both inflation and political pressure, reinforcing that monetary policy uncertainty remains a key business risk.
