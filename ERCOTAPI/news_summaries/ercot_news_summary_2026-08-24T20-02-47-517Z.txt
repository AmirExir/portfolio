- ERCOT posted a cluster of market-rule changes centered on fuel cost recovery and reliability procurement: NPRR1346 would raise the Fuel Oil Price transportation adder from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, and NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost process sunset from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030—together signaling continued accommodation for thermal unit fuel logistics and cost pass-through during tight system conditions. (ERCOT NPRR1346, NPRR1345)
- ERCOT opened several market design changes affecting DAM behavior and reliability procurement: NPRR1343 proposes a fee on inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market, while NPRR1342 creates a new mitigated offer cap process for Contract for Capacity resources—both aimed at improving market efficiency while preserving reliability procurement discipline. (ERCOT NPRR1343, NPRR1342)
- Governance/process changes are also advancing: NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, tightening formal approval and change control around market testing and implementation governance. (ERCOT NPRR1344)
- Large load interconnection reform remains a major planning theme: PGRR145 would create a transitional “Batch Zero” process so ERCOT can study qualifying large loads on a system-wide basis, a sign that data center and other large-demand requests are now large enough to require portfolio-level reliability screening rather than case-by-case treatment. (ERCOT PGRR145)
- A companion large-load planning change, PGRR144, would clarify dynamic model submission and review requirements for large loads including Large Computational Loads, which should improve transient stability studies, fault response assessment, and visibility into how new demand behaves during disturbances. (ERCOT PGRR144)
- ERCOT also posted NOGRR289 to give certain Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to complete cooling-load ride-through compliance, suggesting ERCOT is balancing rapid energization of large loads with staged technical compliance—helpful for project execution, but with clear reliability implications if load behavior during disturbances is not fully hardened on day one. (ERCOT NOGRR289)
- Storage participation in reliability services continues to expand: NOGRR288, related to NPRR1340, adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, which should widen the pool of fast-response reliability capacity and improve operational flexibility during scarcity or ramping events. (ERCOT NOGRR288)
- Transmission and operating reliability changes include NOGRR286, which adds 765-kV operational voltage limits, indicating ERCOT is preparing its operating framework for extra-high-voltage facilities that could support major backbone transmission expansion and future power transfers. (ERCOT NOGRR286)
- Load shed governance is being refined through NOGRR287, which would guide TOs and TDSPs to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads during load shed planning—an operationally important step to make rotating outages more targeted and reduce unintended impacts to critical services. (ERCOT NOGRR287)
- Generator and planning transparency updates continue: PGRR132 clarifies that new Resources must interconnect under a new SGIA, and PGRR131 would require interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators, improving comparability of project costs and visibility into grid upgrade burdens for new supply. (ERCOT PGRR132, PGRR131)
- Reliability standards alignment is also visible in PGRR147, which aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC Category P6/N-1-1 expectations, potentially affecting how transmission projects and outage conditions are assessed in planning studies. (ERCOT PGRR147)
- Several additional ERCOT pages and comment files were updated alongside these rulemakings—including workshop pages, protocol libraries, submission process pages, and stakeholder comment documents on PGRR145 from TEBA and Vistra; while mostly administrative or not fully readable here, their posting indicates active stakeholder debate over how ERCOT should queue, screen, and study large load interconnections. (ERCOT Workshops; ERCOT PGRR145 stakeholder comment files)
