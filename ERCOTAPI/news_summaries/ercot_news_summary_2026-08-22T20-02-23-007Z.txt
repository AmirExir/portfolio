- ERCOT posted NPRR1346 to raise the Fuel Oil Price transportation-cost adder from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, a targeted change that would improve cost recovery assumptions for oil-fired generation and backup fuel usage during stressed system conditions; the practical effect is modestly higher recognized emergency fuel costs, with implications for scarcity-period offer economics and reliability support from dual-fuel units. (ERCOT NPRR1346)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1345 to extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost Process sunset from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030, signaling ERCOT expects fuel-cost volatility and emergency fuel procurement issues to remain operationally relevant for several more years; this preserves a key reliability backstop for generators facing atypical fuel costs and reduces near-term policy uncertainty around cost recovery. (ERCOT NPRR1345)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 and NPRR1342 with market design implications: NPRR1343 would add a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in DAM to curb uneconomic congestion-trading behavior, while NPRR1342 would create a new mitigated offer cap process for Contract for Capacity resources, tightening how reliability-procured resources are priced into the market. (ERCOT NPRR1343; ERCOT NPRR1342)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, effectively elevating testing and approval governance into the formal rule structure; this should standardize market-change implementation, improve stakeholder visibility, and reduce ambiguity around go-live readiness for future system and market upgrades. (ERCOT NPRR1344)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to allow some Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet cooling-load ride-through requirements, highlighting continuing rule refinement for large load interconnection; this may ease near-term onboarding for data-center-type loads, but it also raises planning and operational questions about how quickly new large loads must fully perform through grid disturbances. (ERCOT NOGRR289)
- ERCOT continues building a more formal large-load framework through PGRR145 and PGRR144: PGRR145 creates a “Batch Zero” process to study reliability impacts of mature large-load interconnection requests on a system-wide basis, while PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission requirements for Large Loads including Large Computational Loads; together they point to tighter screening of load growth, more rigorous modeling, and a stronger link between interconnection timing and system reliability. (ERCOT PGRR145; ERCOT PGRR144)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR288 to add Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, extending storage’s role in reliability operations; this should improve ancillary service flexibility, diversify reserve supply, and support faster-response reliability tools as ERCOT integrates more inverter-based resources. (ERCOT NOGRR288)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 establishing 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important signal that ultra-high-voltage infrastructure is becoming a more active operational planning topic; the significance is system-wide, as 765-kV standards shape transmission expansion assumptions, reactive support needs, and future bulk-transfer capability. (ERCOT NOGRR286)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 and PGRR147, both focused on reliability standards during stressed transmission conditions: NOGRR287 would improve load-shed coordination to minimize overlap with essential loads, while PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for P6/N-1-1 contingencies; together they sharpen emergency operations and contingency planning rules. (ERCOT NOGRR287; ERCOT PGRR147)
- ERCOT posted generator interconnection and planning-governance items including PGRR132, requiring new resources to interconnect via a new SGIA, PGRR131 on mandatory reporting of transmission-connected generator interconnection costs, and PGRR142 defining “in-kind” generation; these changes improve interconnection clarity, cost transparency, and consistency in determining whether project changes trigger new studies or agreements. (ERCOT PGRR132; ERCOT PGRR131; ERCOT PGRR142)
- ERCOT posted PGRR146 to shift the large generator and Large Load stability assessment cycle from quarterly to every four months and rename the assessment, indicating an attempt to better align study cadence with the volume of new interconnection activity while preserving planning oversight; for developers, this could affect study timing and milestone expectations. (ERCOT PGRR146)
- ERCOT posted OBDRR055 revising the Non-Spinning Reserve deployment and recall procedure for RTC+B, indicating continued fine-tuning of how reserves are actually deployed in real-time tools; while the full operational details are not fully readable here, the title suggests a significant implementation alignment between reserve procedures and ERCOT’s evolving real-time commitment/dispatch framework. (ERCOT OBDRR055)
