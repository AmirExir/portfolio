- Texas remains a focal point for AI-driven load growth, with multiple reports pointing to data centers chasing scarce power and increasingly considering dedicated or behind-the-meter supply; for ERCOT, that reinforces pressure on large-load interconnection processes, transmission planning, and assessment of how much new demand can rely on grid service versus self-supply. (Business Insider; SemiAnalysis; CNBC)

- New reporting says Microsoft is adding major Texas data-center capacity in Pecos and has arranged long-term natural-gas-fired supply, while CNBC reports GE Vernova turbines are being used for Texas data-center power builds; the ERCOT implication is faster growth in dispatchable private generation tied to hyperscale load, especially in West Texas where transmission and deliverability constraints already matter. (Yahoo Finance; CNBC)

- The same data-center buildout is increasingly an operational reliability issue, not just a planning issue: heat waves and severe weather are being cited as direct risks to high-density computing facilities, which in ERCOT means more weather-sensitive peak demand, stronger need for flexible load response, and greater scrutiny of resilience standards for large new loads. (CNBC)

- Several articles highlight growing public and political backlash against data-center development, including in Texas discussions; for ERCOT stakeholders, that signals rising risk that local permitting, infrastructure siting, and cost-allocation debates could slow load additions or reshape how new transmission and generation support these projects. (Daily Signal; Newsweek; TriplePundit; Biztoc/Chron)

- MIT research cited this week suggests data centers can materially cut costs by shifting consumption away from peak hours; if applied in ERCOT, that supports demand flexibility models where large loads provide curtailment or time-shifting to reduce reserve stress and defer some grid upgrades. (MIT News)

- Reporting on the federal clean-energy tax-credit cutoff says Texas is already seeing sharp price increases in some renewable deals as developers rush to qualify projects; for ERCOT, that could affect the pace and cost of future wind, solar, and storage additions, with knock-on effects for capacity mix, interconnection queues, and long-term adequacy planning. (Times of India/Reuters)

- Broader coverage of grid-connection bottlenecks and pipeline expansion underscores a familiar Texas theme: when power demand grows faster than wires and interconnections, developers turn to gas infrastructure and on-site generation; that dynamic could shift part of ERCOT’s future resource growth outside the traditional market footprint while still affecting reliability, fuel logistics, and transmission congestion. (Marginal Revolution; Insurance Journal; Slashdot/Reuters)
