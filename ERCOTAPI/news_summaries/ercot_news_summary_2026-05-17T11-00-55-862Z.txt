- Texas data-center power demand remains the dominant ERCOT theme: multiple reports point to rapid AI and digital infrastructure expansion, especially in West Texas and the Permian, reinforcing expectations for higher large-load interconnection requests, local transmission upgrades, and tighter scrutiny of deliverability and on-site generation claims. (Yahoo Finance/Oilprice on Fermi; GlobeNewswire on New Era Energy & Digital; WattsUpWithThat; The Verge)

- Fermi said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity and is advancing permits for roughly 11 GW of future Texas AI-campus development, signaling that large new loads are increasingly pairing with dedicated supply rather than relying solely on ERCOT’s existing fleet; the key grid issue is whether these projects are truly islanded/behind-the-meter or still create reliability and transmission consequences for the bulk system. (Yahoo Finance/Oilprice)

- New Era Energy & Digital highlighted strategic progress on integrated power assets in the Permian Basin, another sign that developers are targeting ERCOT’s load-growth hotspots with combined power-and-compute models; for ERCOT planners, this increases the need to distinguish speculative announcements from financeable projects that will actually hit interconnection queues and drive TSP upgrades. (GlobeNewswire)

- A Hill County moratorium on new data center and energy storage development shows local siting and community pushback are becoming a non-market constraint in Texas; even where ERCOT can accommodate load, county-level resistance could slow project timing, alter geography, and shift where transmission and generation investment ultimately lands. (Insurance Journal)

- National backlash over data centers’ power and water use is becoming an ERCOT-relevant governance risk: coverage on local opposition, water conflicts, and public preference for other infrastructure over AI campuses suggests Texas projects may face more permitting friction, tougher local politics, and greater pressure to prove economic and reliability benefits. (Fortune on water use; Forbes; The Verge; Mediaite)

- Chevron’s reported pursuit of a major Texas tax break for a power plant dedicated to a data center underscores a growing policy question for ERCOT: whether large customers should be served through merchant grid infrastructure, dedicated self-supply, or subsidized bespoke generation—and who bears the reliability and cost-allocation consequences when those lines blur. (Mother Jones/WIRED)

- Emerging concepts like “tiny” distributed home-based data centers are still more conceptual than system-relevant for ERCOT, but they point toward a longer-term possibility of flexible, geographically dispersed computing load; if such models scale, they could eventually interact with demand response, distributed energy resources, and retail tariff design rather than drive traditional bulk interconnection needs. (Fortune; Yahoo Finance republish)

- Texas-facing clean firming options continue to gain investor attention alongside AI load growth, with Fervo’s high-profile geothermal IPO reinforcing that dispatchable low-carbon resources may become more valuable in ERCOT if data-center demand keeps accelerating and buyers seek cleaner 24/7 supply portfolios. (Fortune on Fervo)
