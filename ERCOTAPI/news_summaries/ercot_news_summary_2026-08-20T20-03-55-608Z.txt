- ERCOT posted a new cluster of market-rule revisions focused on fuel cost recovery and reliability procurement: NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost Process sunset from 2027 to 2030, while NPRR1346 would double the transportation adder in the Fuel Oil Price from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal. The combined effect is to preserve and increase cost recognition for units burning expensive backup fuels, which can matter during tight system conditions or fuel disruptions. Longer term, this signals ERCOT is still hardening reliability tools around thermal unit fuel assurance rather than winding them down. (ERCOT NPRR1345, NPRR1346)

- ERCOT also posted market design changes aimed at improving price formation and DAM efficiency: NPRR1343 proposes a fee on inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market, which could discourage congestion gaming or low-value bid volume and improve DAM execution. For traders and congestion participants, that raises the cost of submitting uneconomic bids; for the market overall, it suggests ERCOT is tightening transaction quality as congestion and hedging complexity grow. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- A new reliability-procurement pricing change was posted under NPRR1342, creating a specific Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT’s authority. That matters because mitigation rules directly affect whether emergency or reliability-procured capacity can recover costs without distorting scarcity pricing. The governance implication is ERCOT is adding more formal rules around out-of-market reliability commitments as those tools become more operationally important. (ERCOT NPRR1342)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19. What happened is mostly procedural, but the impact is stronger governance: testing rules for market systems would sit directly in Protocols, making approval and change control more standardized. That should reduce ambiguity around implementation readiness for future market-rule and systems changes. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- Large load policy remains a top ERCOT reliability theme: PGRR145 would create a “Batch Zero” process to study mature large-load interconnection requests on a system-wide basis, and PGRR144 would tighten dynamic model submission requirements for Large Loads, including Large Computational Loads. Together, these changes show ERCOT is moving from case-by-case review toward portfolio-level planning and better load modeling, which is critical as data centers and other large loads can materially affect transmission needs, stability margins, and local reliability. (ERCOT PGRR145, PGRR144)

- On the same large-load front, NOGRR282 would establish frequency and voltage ride-through requirements for Large Computational Loads, while NOGRR289 would give certain LCL cooling loads up to 365 days after in-service to comply. What happened is ERCOT is formalizing performance expectations but allowing phased compliance where facility design makes immediate conformity difficult. Operationally, this is aimed at preventing abrupt load drop-offs during grid disturbances; from a planning perspective, it shows ERCOT is balancing grid-support obligations with the commercial reality of rapid large-load buildout. (ERCOT NOGRR282, NOGRR289)

- ERCOT posted PGRR146 to reduce the cadence of stability assessments for planned large generators and Large Loads from quarterly to every four months, and to rename the assessment. That likely eases administrative burden while preserving recurring screening of a fast-changing interconnection queue. The key implication is ERCOT is refining—not removing—oversight as generator and large-load additions continue to reshape system stability risk. (ERCOT PGRR146)

- ERCOT advanced transmission and operating-guide changes with direct reliability relevance: NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits, and NOGRR280 removes redundant communications language for CREZ circuits and stations where requirements already exist elsewhere. The first supports operation of higher-voltage infrastructure likely tied to major transfer capability expansion; the second is more housekeeping but helps keep operating requirements consistent as the grid becomes more complex. (ERCOT NOGRR286, NOGRR280)

- Load-shed governance is also being refined: NOGRR287 would require TOs and TDSPs to better identify and minimize overlap of essential loads in firm load shed plans. This is operationally important because poor essential-load mapping can either undercut emergency shedding effectiveness or unnecessarily interrupt critical services. The broader implication is ERCOT continues to tighten post-storm emergency procedures at the distribution/transmission interface. (ERCOT NOGRR287)

- Storage and inverter-based resource integration remains active in the rules: NOGRR288 would add Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, and PGRR121 adds model quality and validation requirements for inverter-based ESRs. Together these moves push storage further into reliability service provision while also raising the bar for dynamic model fidelity—important for both real-time operations and planning studies as inverter-based resources make up a larger share of the fleet. (ERCOT NOGRR288, PGRR121)

- ERCOT posted PGRR147 to align allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC expectations for Category P6, N-1-1 contingencies. This is a planning-standard adjustment, but it can materially affect how transmission upgrades, remedial action assumptions, and compliance screens are evaluated. In practice, it may tighten study assumptions and influence when projects or mitigations are needed. (ERCOT PGRR147)

- Several new ballot files were detected for PGRR145, including ROS and TAC ballot spreadsheets, but the files were not machine-readable in this feed. Even without full extraction, their titles indicate the Batch Zero large-load interconnection proposal is moving through ERCOT stakeholder voting, which is a notable governance milestone for one of the most consequential planning changes tied to data-center and other large-load growth. (ERCOT PGRR145 ballot files)
