- ERCOT’s latest outlook is being framed publicly as demand potentially reaching about 367,790 MW by 2032—more than 4x today’s record peak—driven largely by data centers and other large loads, signaling an unprecedented scale challenge for generation, transmission, interconnection, and system planning if even part of that pipeline materializes. (Rigzone; Business Insider)

- The data-center buildout is increasingly shifting from “grid-served load” to hybrid and behind-the-meter models, with reports that operators are pairing batteries with on-site natural gas and one Texas project ordering a 790 MW off-grid power package; for ERCOT, that points to faster load deployment but also harder visibility into operational risk, fuel dependence, emissions, and future market participation. (Fortune; Financial Post; GlobeNewswire/Wärtsilä)

- Financing strain is emerging around Texas data-center power deals, including reports that lenders struggled to syndicate Oracle-linked Texas data-center loans and that startup Fermi is in turmoil; that suggests some announced large-load projects may slip, resize, or fail, which matters because ERCOT planners must distinguish speculative queue demand from truly financeable load. (Alltoc/WSJ summary; Semafor; Futurism)

- Semiconductor and AI infrastructure demand remains a strong read-through for Texas power growth: Intel and Texas Instruments both cited AI/data-center strength, reinforcing expectations for continued hyperscale expansion and associated ERCOT load growth, especially in regions competing for chip, cloud, and digital infrastructure investment. (TechCrunch; CNBC; Manufacturing Dive; CNA)

- The surge in AI-related demand is also reviving interest in firm power options—including gas-backed storage and nuclear developers raising capital—which increases the odds that ERCOT will see more proposals for dispatchable, co-located, or dedicated supply resources tied to large loads rather than traditional fully grid-dependent service. (Fortune; Financial Post; TechCrunch)

- Texas is increasingly being cast as the key stress test for the AI-power buildout, but the emerging split between headline demand announcements and real-world execution means the core ERCOT issue is no longer just “how much load is coming,” but which projects are credible, when they energize, and what network upgrades or operational constraints they impose. (Business Insider; Rigzone; Semafor)
