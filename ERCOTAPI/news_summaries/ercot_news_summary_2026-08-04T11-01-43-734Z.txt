- ERCOT posted a coordinated market-rule package to let Energy Storage Resources provide Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service: NPRR1340 adds ESR participation to DRRS and related NOGRR288 aligns operating-guide language. This could expand fast-response reserve supply, improve ancillary-service flexibility, and further integrate batteries into reliability operations. Planning implication: ERCOT is continuing to redesign reliability products around storage rather than thermal-only assumptions. (ERCOT NPRR1340; NOGRR288)

- ERCOT posted PGRR145 creating a “Batch Zero” process for Large Load interconnections, a major procedural step for studying high-volume large-load requests on a system-wide basis. Grid impact: this should improve visibility into cumulative reliability impacts from data centers, crypto, and other large loads rather than evaluating them only one-by-one. Planning implication: ERCOT is moving toward more centralized queue discipline and portfolio-style screening of large-load demand growth. (ERCOT PGRR145)

- ERCOT also posted PGRR144 on dynamic model submission requirements for Large Loads, including Large Computational Loads, complementing the Batch Zero effort. Operational impact: better dynamic models should improve transient and voltage analysis for large electronically connected loads and reduce study uncertainty. Governance implication: ERCOT is tightening technical entry requirements for large-load interconnection as these loads become a system reliability issue, not just a customer service issue. (ERCOT PGRR144)

- A new TAC report for NOGRR287 shows recommended approval for load-shed provisions aimed at identifying and minimizing overlap of essential loads. Reliability impact: this would help transmission operators and TDSPs refine firm load shed implementation so critical facilities are less likely to be interrupted unnecessarily during emergency curtailments. Governance implication: post-2021 resilience work is continuing to shift from broad emergency rules toward more granular execution standards. (ERCOT NOGRR287 TAC Report 07/29/2026)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to extend compliance timing for Large Computational Load cooling load ride-through, allowing up to 365 days after in-service under specified conditions. Operational impact: this may ease immediate interconnection barriers for new LCL facilities, but it also signals ERCOT is balancing rapid load growth with the need for disturbance ride-through performance. Planning implication: large-load reliability standards are still being calibrated as interconnection volume accelerates. (ERCOT NOGRR289)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 establishing 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important marker for extra-high-voltage system buildout. Grid impact: formal operating criteria at 765 kV support reliable use of new bulk transmission infrastructure and indicate planning is advancing toward higher-capacity backbone solutions. This has long-term implications for moving generation and serving load growth across constrained regions. (ERCOT NOGRR286)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 proposing a Day-Ahead Market bid fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids. Market impact: the change appears aimed at reducing uneconomic congestion-trading behavior and improving DAM efficiency. Planning implication: ERCOT continues to tune congestion-right and day-ahead market design to reduce friction and uplift risks as transmission constraints evolve. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a new Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured through emergency authority. Market/reliability impact: clearer offer-cap treatment could affect how emergency or backstop capacity is bid and mitigated when ERCOT needs out-of-market reliability support. Governance implication: ERCOT is building more explicit market rules around reliability procurement tools that sit near the boundary between competitive markets and emergency intervention. (ERCOT NPRR1342)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into the Protocols. Governance impact: this standardizes approval and change control for market testing, giving stakeholders more formal oversight of how market changes are validated before implementation. Operationally, it should reduce ambiguity around testing requirements for future system and rule changes. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- Additional posted revisions were more administrative but still relevant to market transparency and interconnection governance: PGRR131 adds interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators; PGRR132 clarifies new resources must interconnect via a new SGIA; PGRR143 moves planning schedule postings to the public ERCOT website. Together these changes improve comparability, process clarity, and transparency for developers navigating the ERCOT queue and study process. (ERCOT PGRR131; PGRR132; PGRR143)
