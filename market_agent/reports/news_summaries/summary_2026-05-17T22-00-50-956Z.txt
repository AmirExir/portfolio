- **Oil is the dominant macro story:** With the Strait of Hormuz still largely closed, analysts warn crude could spike to **$130–$140/barrel** in June, raising the risk of panic buying and a broader inflation shock.

- **The energy squeeze is spreading beyond gasoline:** Companies are flagging **synthetic motor oil shortages and higher lubricant costs**, showing how commodity disruptions are now filtering into everyday maintenance and transport costs.

- **Farmers are getting hit from both sides:** Rising **diesel and fertilizer prices** are worsening pressure on U.S. agriculture during planting season, a sign that the oil shock is feeding directly into food production economics.

- **Utility M&A may be heating up:** **NextEra Energy is reportedly nearing a deal for Dominion**, a potentially significant consolidation move in the power sector as utilities race to meet growing electricity demand.

- **AI remains a tale of enthusiasm and scrutiny:**  
  - **Microsoft** won public backing from **Pershing Square**, underscoring investor confidence in large-cap AI platforms.  
  - **Cerebras**, fresh off a blockbuster IPO, highlights how capital-intensive AI infrastructure remains despite huge market appetite.  
  - Meanwhile, renewed scrutiny around **OpenAI CEO Sam Altman** shows governance and trust issues are becoming part of the AI investment debate.

- **Transport disruption is becoming an economic risk:** A **Long Island Rail Road strike** and fresh criticism of **Amtrak/Penn Station planning** point to mounting infrastructure and labor fragility in a key commuter corridor.

- **Luxury demand still draws crowds — and operational risk:** A limited-edition **Swatch x Audemars Piguet launch** in Houston triggered disorder and a store closure, a small but vivid sign that scarcity-driven consumer demand remains strong.

- **Politics and branding continue to collide with business:** “**Trump Mobile**” is facing criticism over promotional missteps, illustrating the reputational and execution risks of highly politicized consumer products.

- **Inflation is still the shadow over consumers:** Commentary on the Fed’s inflation outlook suggests cost-of-living pressures remain central, especially for households dependent on indexed benefits like Social Security.

- **Bottom line:** Today’s business picture is defined by **energy-driven cost pressure**, **resilient AI/utility dealmaking**, and **operational fragility in transport and supply chains**.
