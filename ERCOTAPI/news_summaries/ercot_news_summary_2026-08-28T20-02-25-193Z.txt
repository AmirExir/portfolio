- ERCOT posted a cluster of market-rule updates centered on fuel cost recovery for thermal reliability: NPRR1346 would raise the fuel-oil transportation adder in the Fuel Oil Price from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, and NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost process sunset from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030. Together, these changes would preserve and expand cost recovery pathways for emergency and high-cost fuel use, supporting generator availability during tight conditions but likely increasing uplift or settlement impacts when invoked. For planners and regulators, this signals ERCOT still sees fuel assurance and scarcity-era thermal performance as an active reliability issue rather than a temporary post-storm fix. (ERCOT NPRR1346, NPRR1345)

- ERCOT advanced another set of revisions aimed at large-load integration, especially data centers and other Large Computational Loads: NOGRR282 would establish frequency and voltage ride-through requirements for LCLs, NOGRR289 would allow up to 365 days after in-service for certain cooling loads to comply, PGRR145 would create a “Batch Zero” process to study mature large-load interconnection requests on a system-wide basis, and PGRR144 would tighten dynamic model submission and review requirements for large loads. Operationally, this points to ERCOT trying to prevent abrupt load loss or unstable performance from very large new demand while also creating a more structured queue/study framework. The broader implication is that ERCOT governance is shifting from case-by-case treatment of hyperscale load growth toward formal planning, performance, and compliance standards. (ERCOT NOGRR282, NOGRR289, PGRR145, PGRR144)

- ERCOT posted reliability and transmission-planning changes that point to a more complex grid topology: NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits, indicating ERCOT is formalizing operating criteria for extra-high-voltage infrastructure, while PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss rules with NERC Category P6/N-1-1 expectations. These updates matter because they sharpen how ERCOT studies severe contingencies and operates around major backbone transmission additions. The planning takeaway is tighter reliability criteria around both future transmission expansion and contingency performance standards. (ERCOT NOGRR286, PGRR147)

- ERCOT continues refining emergency operations and reserve deployment rules: OBDRR055 updates the Non-Spinning Reserve deployment/recall procedure to align with RTC+B implementation, and NOGRR288 adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service-related operating guide provisions. That should improve how reserves are committed and deployed in increasingly fast-moving operating conditions and broaden battery participation in reliability products. Market-wise, this is another step toward integrating storage more deeply into core reliability operations rather than treating it as an edge case. (ERCOT OBDRR055, NOGRR288)

- ERCOT also posted governance and process changes that are less visible operationally but important for implementation discipline: NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, standardizing approvals; NPRR1343 would introduce a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in DAM, likely aimed at reducing low-value congestion trading behavior; and PGRR143 would shift annual planning model schedule postings from MIS Secure Area to the public ERCOT website. The combined effect is more formal rule control, cleaner day-ahead market behavior, and somewhat greater transparency in planning administration. (ERCOT NPRR1344, NPRR1343, PGRR143)

- Additional ERCOT guide revisions posted include NOGRR287 on minimizing overlap of essential loads during load shed, NOGRR279 extending high-resolution recording equipment deadlines to Jan. 1, 2029, NOGRR280 removing redundant CREZ communications language, PGRR146 changing the stability assessment cadence for planned large generators and large loads from quarterly to every four months, and PGRR142 defining “in-kind” for generation. While some are administrative, they collectively affect outage preparedness, compliance timing, transmission communications governance, and study expectations for new resources and large demand. (ERCOT NOGRR287, NOGRR279, NOGRR280, PGRR146, PGRR142)
