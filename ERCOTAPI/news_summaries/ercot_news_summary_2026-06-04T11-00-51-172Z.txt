- Texas data-center growth remains the dominant ERCOT theme: multiple reports highlight surging AI-driven load, with ERCOT reportedly advancing a “Batch Zero” screening process to sort feasible large-load interconnection requests amid queues that far exceed near-term deliverability. That points to tighter scrutiny of transmission availability, study assumptions, and energization timing for hyperscale loads. (Slashdot)
- West Texas is emerging as a focal point for new large load on the ERCOT system: coverage of a Fort Stockton-area “Project Horizon” data center says developers are addressing power, water, and community concerns up front, underscoring that future ERCOT load growth will increasingly depend on co-optimizing interconnection, cooling water, local transmission capacity, and public acceptance. (Forbes)
- Water constraints are becoming a material grid-planning issue for Texas data centers, not just a local permitting concern. Broader reporting on hyperscaler efforts to reduce water use suggests siting and operating strategy in ERCOT will increasingly favor designs that can minimize water intensity during drought and extreme heat, when both cooling demand and system stress are elevated. (Wired)
- New utility-scale solar continues to scale into ERCOT load growth: Vesper Energy said it reached financial close on another large Texas solar project, reinforcing that solar remains the fastest deployable bulk supply option for near-term demand growth, though its reliability value still depends on storage, transmission deliverability, and thermal fleet availability during evening ramps. (Electrek)
- Storage and behind-the-meter power infrastructure continue to converge with data-center development. T1 Energy’s acquisition of KORE Power signals ongoing interest in pairing BESS manufacturing/infrastructure with digital-load buildout, a trend that could improve large-load flexibility and peak management in ERCOT if projects include co-located storage or controllable on-site power. (GlobeNewswire)
- Texas heat and drought risk are again being flagged as power-system stressors, reinforcing ERCOT’s ongoing summer reliability challenge: weather volatility raises the chance of higher peaks, tighter operating reserves, and localized transmission constraints, especially as new large loads seek service before matching generation and wires upgrades are in place. (PRNewswire)
