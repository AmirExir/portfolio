- Hill County approved a one-year moratorium on new rural data center projects, a notable local pushback against rapid AI-load expansion in Texas; legal uncertainty remains because state officials argue counties may lack authority to impose such bans. For ERCOT, this highlights rising siting friction for large loads even as interconnection demand keeps growing. (Tom's Hardware UK / Slashdot.org / Insurance Journal)

- The Hill County action also signals that large-load development risk is no longer just about power availability and transmission access; local land-use, water, and community acceptance are becoming potential gating items that could delay ERCOT load forecasts, TSP planning assumptions, and generator/load co-location strategies. (Tom's Hardware UK / Insurance Journal)

- Broader data-center backlash is hardening nationally around power and water use, and Texas is now part of that narrative. For ERCOT stakeholders, that raises the odds of more local opposition, tighter scrutiny of behind-the-meter or dedicated-generation arrangements, and possible state-level governance responses to standardize how large loads are approved. (Fortune / Forbes / The Verge / Wattsupwiththat.com)

- Multiple reports point to AI infrastructure supply-chain constraints, including severe fiber-optic shortages and long lead times. While not ERCOT-specific, these bottlenecks could slow data-center buildout timing and therefore shift the pace of expected large-load additions, telecom upgrades, and associated transmission/service planning in ERCOT. (Tom's Hardware UK)

- New Era Energy & Digital said it is developing integrated power and digital infrastructure in the Permian Basin, reinforcing the theme of power-backed computing projects in West Texas. If projects advance, ERCOT implications include added large-load growth in a region already sensitive to transmission constraints, deliverability, and co-development of private generation. (GlobeNewswire)

- Fermi said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity and is advancing permitting for roughly 11 GW of future development tied to a Texas AI power campus. Even if early-stage, the scale is material for ERCOT: projects of this size can affect interconnection queues, local reliability studies, fuel/infrastructure needs, and market expectations around dedicated supply for hyperscale loads. (Yahoo Entertainment)

- Reporting on Chevron seeking a large Texas tax break for a power plant dedicated to a data center underscores a key emerging ERCOT issue: whether new generation for hyperscale loads will be grid-serving, islanded, or effectively reserved for single customers. That has implications for resource adequacy, cost allocation, emissions profile, and market design fairness. (Mother Jones)

- Several items reinforce that AI/data-center expansion is becoming a core Texas power-planning issue rather than just a tech story. The combined message for ERCOT is clear: large-load forecasting, interconnection policy, local opposition risk, and the treatment of co-located or dedicated generation are likely to remain central operational and governance questions. (Tom's Hardware UK / GlobeNewswire / Yahoo Entertainment / Mother Jones)
