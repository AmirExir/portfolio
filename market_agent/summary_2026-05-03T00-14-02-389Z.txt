- **Apple steadies Big Tech sentiment:** Apple gave a **strong revenue outlook** despite **chip-cost pressure** and ongoing **Mac shortages**, reinforcing investor confidence that top-tier tech can still grow through supply constraints.

- **AI’s real moat looks like infrastructure:** Google’s post-earnings surge underscored a broader theme: **compute capacity and capital spending** may matter as much as model quality. That supports the view that AI winners will be those with the deepest infrastructure.

- **Pentagon deepens AI ties:** Leading AI firms including **Microsoft, Amazon, and Google** agreed to deploy technology in **classified Defense networks**, a sign that government demand could become a larger and more strategic AI revenue stream.

- **Spirit Airlines faces a cliff-edge moment:** Spirit could **shut down as soon as Saturday** after talks over a **$500 million aid package** stalled. It’s a stark reminder of how fragile balance sheets remain in lower-cost, highly leveraged travel businesses.

- **GameStop reportedly eyes eBay:** A potential **GameStop bid for eBay** would be a surprising, high-profile deal and raises questions about strategy, financing, and whether meme-era companies can reinvent themselves through acquisition.

- **Oil outlook remains volatile:** Even as oil cooled enough to help stocks today, traders are still betting U.S. crude could **break above wartime highs** if geopolitical conflict drags on. For investors, that means inflation and energy risk haven’t disappeared.

- **Currency markets are watching Japan closely:** After a reported **$34.5 billion intervention**, traders are alert for more moves to support the **yen**, highlighting rising policy activism in global FX markets.

- **Consumer trends keep shifting in strange ways:** Demand effects from **GLP-1 drugs** are showing up in unexpected corners, including a reported boost to **gum sales**, showing how health-tech adoption can ripple across consumer staples.

- **Executive pay remains a flashpoint:** Tesla disclosed Elon Musk’s 2025 compensation value at roughly **$158.4 billion**, keeping the spotlight on outsized CEO pay and shareholder governance in founder-led companies.
