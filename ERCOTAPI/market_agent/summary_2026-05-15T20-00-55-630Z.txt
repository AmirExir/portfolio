- Texas data-center growth is emerging as the main ERCOT-adjacent theme: local resistance is rising in North Texas and Hill County, with concerns centered on power demand, energy storage siting, water use, and land-use impacts—signaling growing permitting and siting friction for large new loads and related infrastructure even where ERCOT demand growth is strong. (Insurance Journal; Dallas Observer; The Verge)

- Chevron is reportedly seeking a major Texas tax break to build a dedicated power plant for a data center, underscoring a likely shift toward behind-the-meter or directly paired generation for hyperscale loads rather than relying solely on grid-supplied service. For ERCOT, that points to tougher questions around interconnection, deliverability, backup grid dependence, emissions, and whether large loads socialize network costs while self-supplying energy. (Mother Jones)

- EIA now forecasts utility-scale solar generation in ERCOT will exceed coal generation in 2026 for the first time, reinforcing the system’s rapid fuel-mix transition. Operationally, that increases the importance of thermal flexibility, storage, ancillary services, transmission capability, and scarcity-period reliability as coal’s share falls and solar’s midday dominance grows. (EIA)

- A new South Texas modular AI data-center expansion of about 6 MW was announced, small in isolation but directionally consistent with a broader wave of incremental large-load additions across ERCOT. The planning implication is less about this single project and more about cumulative load growth, especially where clusters of smaller AI or digital-infrastructure sites can tighten local delivery constraints before they appear material at the system level. (PRNewswire)

- Multiple national stories on data-center load, community backlash, and infrastructure stress strengthen the case that ERCOT planners, TSPs, and regulators will face more disputes over who pays for upgrades, how speculative loads are screened, and how to prioritize interconnections for genuinely financeable projects. This is becoming as much a governance and queue-management issue as a pure load-growth story. (The Verge; Forbes; Fortune; Insurance Journal)

- Cyber risk remains a grid-relevant watch item after Foxconn confirmed a cyberattack affecting North American operations; while not an ERCOT market event, it highlights supply-chain vulnerability for critical electrical equipment, electronics, and digital infrastructure serving large loads and grid projects. Any disruption in transformer, controls, or server-related supply chains could indirectly affect ERCOT build timelines and outage planning. (MacDailyNews; Times of India)

- Fervo’s high-profile IPO adds visibility to dispatchable geothermal as a potential future complement to ERCOT’s solar-heavy growth, even though the cited commercial buildout is outside Texas. For ERCOT long-term planning, the significance is the continued search for clean firm capacity options that can serve reliability needs and potentially support data-center-driven demand growth without worsening peak net-load risk. (Fortune)

- Texas awarded USA Rare Earth a $14.2 million state grant for its Round Top project, a manufacturing/supply-chain development rather than an immediate ERCOT system event, but still relevant for long-range grid planning because mineral processing and related industrial expansion can add regional load and support domestic supply chains for electrification, grid equipment, and advanced technologies. (GlobeNewswire)
