- Texas data-center growth is increasingly colliding with local permitting and land-use resistance: Hill County approved a one-year rural data-center moratorium, highlighting a growing mismatch between hyperscale load interest and local siting tolerance in areas targeted for cheaper land and easier transmission access. For ERCOT, that raises the odds of load shifting to other counties, more speculative interconnection requests, and added uncertainty in transmission planning for large load pockets. (Tom's Hardware UK; Insurance Journal)

- Chevron is reportedly seeking a major Texas tax break to build a power plant dedicated to serving a data center, underscoring the “bring your own power” model gaining traction for large loads. The ERCOT implication is that new data-center demand may increasingly pair with behind-the-meter or directly contracted generation, which could reduce near-term grid procurement needs in some zones but complicate visibility, operational modeling, fuel-risk assessment, and market impacts if those resources interact with the bulk system. (Grist; Mother Jones)

- NRG’s new CEO is signaling two themes highly relevant to ERCOT: custom power solutions for AI-driven large loads and broader use of virtual power plants to manage affordability. That points to a market shift toward co-located supply, retail-structured reliability products, and more distributed demand flexibility—potentially important for ERCOT reserve margins, peak management, and retail/wholesale coordination as large loads grow faster than traditional infrastructure timelines. (Fortune)

- Several Texas-linked reports reinforce that large load development is outpacing supporting infrastructure, not just generation: one article cites a 4,032-GPU Texas facility using liquid cooling, while another says AI data centers require dramatically more fiber and are stretching supply chains. For ERCOT, this suggests data-center interconnection viability will increasingly depend on coordinated power, telecom, water, and equipment delivery timelines, making announced projects less certain than headline MW figures imply. (Crypto Briefing; Tom's Hardware UK)

- New Era Energy & Digital said it is advancing integrated power and digital infrastructure in the Permian Basin, while Fermi America said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity and is permitting roughly 11 GW of future Texas development tied to its AI power campus strategy. Even allowing for promotional overstatement, these announcements signal continued pressure on ERCOT to evaluate very large load and self-supplied generation concepts in West Texas, with major implications for queue management, transmission expansion, and reliability studies. (GlobeNewswire; Yahoo Finance/OilPrice)

- The broader political and public narrative around data-center power demand in Texas is intensifying, with debates over subsidies, grid strain, and whether data centers are being unfairly blamed. For ERCOT stakeholders, that matters because public backlash can influence legislation, county action, interconnection policy, and cost-allocation debates—especially around who pays for generation, wires, and system upgrades needed to serve new large loads. (Mediaite; Watts Up With That; The Verge)
