- ERCOT posted market-rule changes to price Settlement Only Distribution Generators and Settlement Only Transmission Generators at nodal energy prices instead of load-zone prices under NPRR917. That would sharpen local price signals for behind-the-meter/settlement-only generation, improve settlement accuracy in constrained areas, and could influence dispatch economics and siting incentives for small or non-traditional resources. (NPRR917, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1340 and related NOGRR288 to allow Energy Storage Resources to participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. This expands fast-response reserve capability, improves operational flexibility during tight conditions, and signals continued integration of batteries into core reliability products rather than treating them as energy-only assets. (NPRR1340/NOGRR288, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a mitigated offer cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under emergency authority. The change is aimed at controlling scarcity-era offer behavior for stopgap reliability resources while preserving ERCOT’s ability to procure capacity when needed. (NPRR1342, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to add a Day-Ahead Market bid fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids. The likely impact is reduced congestion-rights gaming or low-value bid volume, cleaner DAM outcomes, and lower administrative friction in transmission congestion hedging. (NPRR1343, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19. This is mainly governance and process standardization, but it matters because market-system testing rules would then follow the formal Protocol approval path, giving participants more transparency and procedural certainty ahead of major market changes. (NPRR1344, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to give some Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet cooling-load ride-through requirements. What happened: ERCOT is proposing a delayed compliance path for certain LCLs; impact: this could ease near-term data-center interconnection timing while temporarily accepting more operational risk from load drop-off during disturbances; implication: it highlights ERCOT’s effort to balance rapid large-load growth with stricter performance standards needed for system stability. (NOGRR289, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted PGRR144 to clarify dynamic model submission and review requirements for large loads, including Large Computational Loads. Better load modeling should improve transient and voltage-stability studies, reduce planning uncertainty tied to hyperscale load interconnections, and support more defensible transmission and interconnection decisions. (PGRR144, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 to add 765-kV operational voltage limits. That is a significant transmission-planning and operations signal, indicating ERCOT is formalizing procedures for extra-high-voltage infrastructure that can support long-distance bulk transfer capability and future backbone expansion. (NOGRR286, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 on load-shed provisions to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. The operational goal is more targeted firm load shed during emergencies, which could reduce collateral impacts to critical services and improve blackout-management discipline during extreme reliability events. (NOGRR287, ERCOT)
- ERCOT posted several planning-guide updates tied to interconnection transparency and study governance: PGRR131 on mandatory interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators, PGRR132 on requiring new resources to interconnect via a new SGIA, and PGRR143 on moving planning model schedule postings to the public website. Together these point to a more standardized and transparent generator interconnection framework as ERCOT manages rapid resource growth. (PGRR131/PGRR132/PGRR143, ERCOT)
- ERCOT also posted PGRR146, PGRR147, and PGRR121 affecting planning study cadence, contingency/load-loss criteria, and inverter-based ESR model validation. Combined, these changes tighten planning assumptions around stability, NERC alignment, and battery performance—important for reliability as inverter-based resources and large loads become a larger share of the ERCOT system. (PGRR146/PGRR147/PGRR121, ERCOT)
