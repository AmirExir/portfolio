- **Oil and geopolitics are back in control of markets:** Tensions in the Strait of Hormuz pushed crude sharply higher after reports of Iranian drone attacks on a tanker and U.S. efforts to guide/evacuate ships. The key implication: energy prices and shipping risk are again major macro variables.

- **Stocks are wobbling as higher oil revives inflation fears:** Dow futures fell and broader markets turned cautious, even after recent record highs. Investors appear to be reassessing whether energy-driven inflation could complicate the rate outlook.

- **Airlines face a double hit from fuel and disruption:** Reports suggest carriers are cutting flights as Middle East risks raise fuel shortage concerns and disrupt travel planning. This is a fresh pressure point for an industry already operating on thin margins.

- **Ryan Cohen’s GameStop is making a bold play for eBay:** A roughly **$56 billion** takeover bid sent eBay shares surging. Strategically, it’s an ambitious attempt to use eBay’s marketplace scale to challenge Amazon more directly.

- **Dealmaking remains active despite market volatility:**  
  - American Express Global Business Travel agreed to a **$6.3 billion** take-private deal.  
  - UCB is acquiring Candid Therapeutics for **$2 billion upfront**.  
  The takeaway: strategic buyers and private capital are still willing to do big deals in uncertain conditions.

- **AI infrastructure keeps attracting serious capital:**  
  - SAP is acquiring Dremio to strengthen enterprise data integration for AI.  
  - Anthropic is reportedly nearing a **$1.5 billion** joint venture with Wall Street firms.  
  This reinforces that the AI race is shifting from hype to data, tooling, and enterprise distribution.

- **Transportation headlines underscore operational risk:** A United plane reportedly struck a bakery truck while landing near Newark, adding to a run of aviation-related incidents that keep safety and logistics in focus.

- **Bottom line:** Today’s business story is a split screen: **geopolitical risk is rattling oil, airlines, and inflation expectations**, while **M&A and AI investment remain surprisingly resilient underneath the surface**.
