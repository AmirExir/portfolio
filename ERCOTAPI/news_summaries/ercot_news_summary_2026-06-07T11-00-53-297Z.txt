- ERCOT’s large-load interconnection risk is moving from planning issue to near-term operations concern: reports say several proposed data centers and crypto facilities failed voltage/reliability testing ahead of summer, underscoring that not all queued megawatt demand can be connected on the timeline customers want without new grid upgrades or operating constraints. (CNA/Reuters)

- A wave of Texas data center development is accelerating load growth outside major cities, with reports citing 248 planned facilities statewide and multiple projects in unincorporated counties where local governments have limited siting control; for ERCOT, that raises pressure on transmission planning, substation capacity, and large-load screening in rural areas not built for hyperscale demand. (Salon)

- Google’s new Meitner Energy Center in Gray and Roberts Counties points to the model ERCOT will likely see more often: co-located data center load paired with new generation, reportedly over 1 GW of renewables and air-cooled design. The key grid question is whether co-location materially reduces system peaks and interconnection strain, or still requires major transmission and reliability studies. (Google Blog; Crypto Briefing)

- Market and policy debate around Texas data-center rules is intensifying as national coverage contrasts Texas’ more market-based posture with local backlash over land, water, and community impacts. For ERCOT stakeholders, that means large-load interconnection policy is becoming both a reliability and governance issue, not just an economic-development story. (Reason; Salon)

- Community opposition to mega-projects is becoming a material execution risk for Texas power demand forecasts: articles on proposed SpaceX semiconductor/data infrastructure and other hyperscale projects show backlash over tax incentives, infrastructure burden, and local impacts, which could slow load realization versus headline announcements. That matters for ERCOT planning assumptions and the timing of transmission or generation additions. (Business Insider; Tom’s Hardware; Fortune)

- Financing continues to flow into Texas digital-load infrastructure, including Hut 8’s $4.25 billion financing for its Beacon Point data center project, signaling that very large behind-the-meter or front-of-the-meter power-intensive projects are still advancing despite reliability scrutiny. For ERCOT, capital availability suggests the large-load queue will remain robust even as interconnection standards tighten. (PR Newswire; Yahoo Entertainment)

- Battery-storage-related support for Texas grids got a smaller but notable boost from Waymo and B2U’s plan to repurpose EV batteries for grid applications in Texas. The immediate ERCOT impact is limited, but the development reinforces the broader trend toward distributed storage and second-life battery assets supporting local reliability and renewable integration. (GlobeNewswire; Electrek; Gizmodo)
