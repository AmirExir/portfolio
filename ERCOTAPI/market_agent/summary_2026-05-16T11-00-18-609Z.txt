- EIA now forecasts utility-scale solar generation in ERCOT will exceed coal output in 2026 for the first time, signaling a faster shift in the Texas generation mix and greater dependence on storage, flexible gas, and ancillary services to manage net-load ramps and reliability during low-solar hours (EIA)
- Multiple Texas AI/data-center power stories point to accelerating large-load pressure on ERCOT: Fermi says it has secured over 2 GW of generation capacity with ~11 GW of future development in Texas, while AZIO AI announced an additional ~6 MW South Texas deployment—evidence that dedicated power-plus-compute models are moving from concept to execution (Yahoo Entertainment; PRNewswire)
- Chevron is reportedly seeking a major Texas tax break to build a power plant dedicated to a single data center load, underscoring a key ERCOT trend: behind-the-meter or directly paired generation may help serve hyperscale load growth, but it also raises market design, cost-allocation, and system planning questions if large customers bypass traditional grid expansion pathways (Mother Jones)
- Local resistance to data-center and storage siting is emerging inside Texas: Hill County reportedly approved a one-year moratorium on new data center and energy storage projects, highlighting growing land-use and permitting risk that could slow load additions, storage buildout, and associated transmission planning even where ERCOT demand growth is strong (Insurance Journal)
- New Era Energy & Digital highlighted progress on integrated power assets and digital infrastructure in the Permian Basin, reinforcing West Texas as a focal point for co-located power, data, and industrial load growth that could affect congestion, interconnection priorities, and local transmission upgrade needs (GlobeNewswire)
- Broader national coverage of utilities redirecting power toward data centers, public opposition to new AI campuses, and rising scrutiny of water use adds to the Texas planning signal: ERCOT load growth is no longer just an interconnection issue but a governance and social-license issue that could shape county actions, legislative debate, and how future large loads are screened and served (Fortune; Forbes; The Verge)
