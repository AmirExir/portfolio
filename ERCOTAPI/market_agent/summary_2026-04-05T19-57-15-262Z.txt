- Microsoft, Chevron, and Engine No. 1 are in exclusive talks to build a colocated West Texas AI data center and gas-fired generation campus starting around 2.5 GW, with potential expansion to roughly 5 GW. The project would be one of the largest dedicated large-load developments in ERCOT and signals continued hyperscaler interest in self-supplied Texas load growth (Fortune; Financial Post; OilPrice.com).
- For ERCOT, colocating generation with the data center could reduce immediate dependence on an already constrained regional grid, but a multi-gigawatt campus would still have major interconnection, backup, and transmission implications depending on how islanded versus grid-connected the facility is structured. The deal also reinforces a market trend toward behind-the-meter or dedicated thermal supply for AI loads, which could mute some scarcity impacts while complicating load forecasting and market planning (Fortune; Financial Post; OilPrice.com).
- From a planning and reliability standpoint, this scale of new West Texas load raises questions around gas deliverability, transmission upgrades, and whether ERCOT and regulators will need clearer frameworks for large-load interconnections and hybrid campus designs. If replicated, similar projects could materially reshape reserve margins, local congestion patterns, and long-term resource adequacy assumptions in Texas (Fortune; Financial Post; OilPrice.com).
