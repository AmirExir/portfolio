- Multiple outlets reported Intel joining Elon Musk’s proposed “Terafab” semiconductor project in Texas, alongside Tesla and SpaceX, to design, manufacture, and package AI chips for robotics and data center applications. If the project advances, it would reinforce Texas’ position as a magnet for power-intensive industrial and digital infrastructure with potentially very large ERCOT load additions. (TechCrunch, CNA, HWupgrade.it, The Register)

- Google’s plan to use on-site natural-gas generation for a massive Texas data center highlights how new hyperscale loads may increasingly seek dedicated supply rather than relying solely on ERCOT grid service. For ERCOT, that points to continued pressure on interconnection queues, gas-electric coordination, emissions permitting, and potential market impacts if large loads bypass or partially hedge wholesale exposure with behind-the-meter generation. (Yale E360)

- The broader AI buildout continues to signal substantial future power demand growth, with reports of multi-gigawatt compute commitments and new U.S. power investment targeting data-center load. For ERCOT planners, the takeaway is sustained need for transmission expansion, faster large-load interconnection studies, better visibility into co-located generation, and tighter reliability planning around clustered hyperscale demand in Texas. (The Next Web, Financial Post, Fortune, New York Post)
