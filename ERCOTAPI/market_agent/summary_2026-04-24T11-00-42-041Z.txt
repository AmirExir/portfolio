- ERCOT load-growth risk remains the dominant Texas grid story: multiple reports point to explosive data-center and large-load demand, with one outlet citing ERCOT’s preliminary outlook that peak demand could reach 367,790 MW by 2032. The immediate implication is accelerating pressure on transmission expansion, interconnection screening, deliverability, and reliability planning assumptions. (OilPrice)

- Capital is still flowing toward Texas data-center buildout despite grid constraints: financing, insurance, and infrastructure stories around AI campuses, modular compute, and urban-edge facilities indicate developers expect continued ERCOT load additions rather than a slowdown. That supports a view of sustained large-load interconnection queues and tougher questions around who pays for network upgrades and backup supply. (Insurance Journal, Financial Post, Business Insider, TechRadar)

- Texas’ role in the AI hardware supply chain is expanding beyond load growth alone: Q.ANT is opening a U.S. headquarters in Austin and Texas Instruments reported stronger data-center-driven demand. For ERCOT, that reinforces industrial load growth alongside hyperscale demand and suggests more geographically diverse but still power-intensive expansion across Central Texas and existing semiconductor corridors. (GlobeNewswire, CNA, Digitimes)

- Behind-the-meter and dedicated-power strategies for AI campuses are gaining visibility, including gas-fired data-center concepts and startups pursuing gigawatt-scale power solutions. For ERCOT, that raises operational and market design issues around outage coordination, fuel assurance, emissions constraints, and how large flexible or self-supplied loads participate in planning and reliability assessments. (Wired, Fortune, IBTimes, Fortune)

- Crypto-to-AI conversions continue to matter for Texas because they can rapidly repurpose existing high-capacity sites and interconnection positions. Core Scientific’s debt raise to expand data-center operations and Cipher’s continued AI-infrastructure positioning suggest ERCOT should expect more load-profile changes at former mining facilities, with implications for flexibility, local congestion, and transmission utilization. (Decrypt, Cointelegraph, Yahoo Entertainment)

- Data-center operators are increasingly treating onsite batteries and flexible load as grid-facing assets rather than just backup systems. That trend could become material in ERCOT if large loads pair BESS with demand response or ancillary-service participation, potentially helping reliability but also complicating metering, settlement, and operational visibility requirements. (Yahoo Entertainment)

- The governance and execution risk around Texas “AI power” mega-projects remains high: leadership turmoil at Fermi undercuts near-term credibility of some headline-grabbing giga-load proposals, even as the broader demand thesis remains intact. For ERCOT planners, this is a reminder that announced load is not the same as financeable, interconnected, operable demand—and forecast quality will depend on better project screening and milestone discipline. (Fortune, Livemint, Memeorandum)

- Environmental and siting backlash against power-hungry data centers is intensifying nationally, especially where projects rely on new gas generation or create public-health concerns. In Texas, that could translate into tougher permitting, local opposition, and more scrutiny of water, emissions, and transmission impacts—factors that can delay projects even when interconnection demand remains strong. (Wired, Fortune, Gizmodo, Commercial Observer)
