- ERCOT posted a paired market/operating-guide change to let Energy Storage Resources participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service through NPRR1340 and NOGRR288. This would broaden the DRRS supply stack beyond thermal resources, improve fast-response reliability options, and further integrate batteries into scarcity and reserve operations. It also signals ERCOT is continuing to redesign ancillary services around inverter-based flexibility rather than legacy unit types. (ERCOT NPRR1340; NOGRR288)

- ERCOT posted PGRR145 creating a “Batch Zero” process for Large Load Interconnections, a major governance and planning response to the surge in large computational loads. The key impact is earlier system-wide reliability screening for mature large-load projects before they overwhelm local studies or transmission plans. For developers, this points to tighter entry criteria, more queue discipline, and a stronger ERCOT role in sequencing load growth against deliverability and system upgrades. (ERCOT PGRR145)

- ERCOT also posted a new stakeholder filing on PGRR145, “145PGRR-21 Satoshi Energy Comments 032026.” Even without full review of the document, new comments on Batch Zero indicate active stakeholder debate over how ERCOT should prioritize, study, and potentially gate large-load projects—an issue with direct implications for interconnection timing, transmission cost allocation, and reliability risk management. (ERCOT PGRR145 filing)

- ERCOT posted PGRR144 requiring clearer dynamic model submission and review for Large Loads, including Large Computational Loads. Operationally, better load modeling should improve voltage, frequency, and transient stability studies as ERCOT evaluates increasingly power-electronic and rapidly varying demand. In planning terms, this raises the technical bar for new large-load connections and supports more realistic assessment of system impacts before energization. (ERCOT PGRR144)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to allow certain Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to comply with cooling-load ride-through requirements. What happened: ERCOT appears to be offering a phased compliance path for new LCLs. Grid impact: this may ease near-term interconnection friction for data-center-style loads while preserving a defined reliability backstop. Planning/governance implication: ERCOT is formalizing how large loads can connect before full performance compliance, which will be closely watched as a precedent for future load interconnection policy. (ERCOT NOGRR289)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 on load shed provisions to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. The practical impact is improved emergency load-shed execution by reducing the chance that critical facilities are unintentionally concentrated on interrupted circuits. Reliability-wise, this is a post-event resiliency measure that could make rotating outages more targeted and operationally manageable during extreme conditions. (ERCOT NOGRR287)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 adding 765-kV operational voltage limits, a notable sign of transmission expansion into extra-high-voltage operations. This matters because new 765-kV facilities would require tighter operating guidance, voltage control practices, and coordination across planning and real-time operations. Strategically, it points to ERCOT preparing its rulebook for a larger backbone grid capable of moving more remote generation and load-serving power across Texas. (ERCOT NOGRR286)

- ERCOT posted PGRR131 on mandatory interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators and PGRR132 clarifying use of a new Standard Generation Interconnection Agreement. Together, these changes improve transparency and process discipline in generator interconnection, which should help developers benchmark upgrade costs and reduce ambiguity in project development. Over time, this supports more orderly queue management and better planning visibility into where generation can connect economically. (ERCOT PGRR131; PGRR132)

- ERCOT posted PGRR146 to rename and shift the schedule for stability assessments of planned large generators and Large Loads from quarterly to every four months. Operationally this slightly relaxes study cadence, but it also formalizes a recurring review structure for both supply and fast-growing demand additions. The planning implication is that ERCOT is adapting its study processes to handle rising project volume while still maintaining periodic stability oversight. (ERCOT PGRR146)

- ERCOT also posted a new comment file on PGRR146, “146PGRR-09 APA ACP Comments 080526.” Even without full content review, new comments suggest stakeholders remain engaged on how often ERCOT should reassess stability impacts from incoming generation and large loads, with possible implications for project timing, study burden, and planning transparency. (ERCOT PGRR146 filing)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to introduce a fee for inefficient hourly Point-to-Point Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market. Market impact: this could deter low-value congestion positioning behavior and improve DAM efficiency and price formation. For the system, cleaner congestion signals can support better dispatch incentives and more credible transmission value signals for traders, generators, and planners. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 establishing a Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT control-area authority. This is a targeted market design change with potential consequences for how emergency or reliability-driven capacity arrangements are priced and mitigated. Governance-wise, it shows ERCOT continuing to add rules around out-of-market or reliability procurement so those arrangements fit more cleanly within market monitoring and mitigation frameworks. (ERCOT NPRR1342)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into the Protocols. The immediate impact is procedural, but embedding it in Protocols raises the formal approval bar and improves governance consistency for testing market changes. That matters for future market-system deployments because stakeholder oversight and change control become more standardized. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- ERCOT posted several administrative clean-up items—NPRR1341, PGRR139, PGRR143, PGRR147, PGRR137, and NOGRR285—covering glossary moves, posting-location changes, NERC alignment on allowable load loss, and comment-review process updates. These are less headline-driven but still important because they tighten definitional consistency, improve transparency of planning data, and align ERCOT processes with reliability standards and governance expectations. (ERCOT NPRR1341; PGRR139; PGRR143; PGRR147; PGRR137; NOGRR285)
