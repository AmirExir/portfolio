- EIA now forecasts utility-scale solar generation in ERCOT will exceed coal output for the first time in 2026, marking a material shift in the Texas supply stack toward daytime energy abundance and deeper dependence on storage, flexible gas, and transmission to manage net-load ramps and reliability after sunset (EIA Today in Energy)
- Multiple stories point to accelerating large-load growth tied to AI, crypto, and modular data centers in Texas, including a new South Texas deployment and CleanSpark’s report of 585 MW of ERCOT-approved capacity; the core ERCOT implication is faster pressure on interconnection queues, TSP upgrades, deliverability, and large-load screening for both front-of-meter and behind-the-meter configurations (PRNewswire; CleanSpark PRNewswire)
- Community resistance to large data-center development is becoming a real execution risk for Texas load growth, with North Texas opposition highlighting concerns over noise, water, traffic, and land use; for ERCOT, that means some forecasted demand may face siting delays even as planners still need to study transmission and generation needed if projects proceed (Dallas Observer)
- National backlash against AI data centers is broadening beyond power costs to water use and local acceptance, with polling showing strong opposition and multiple reports of infrastructure strain; in ERCOT this raises governance and planning pressure to better distinguish firm grid service, behind-the-meter self-supply, and cost allocation for upgrades triggered by hyperscale loads (Forbes; Fortune; Daily Caller; The Verge)
- The fastest path to serve new data-center demand increasingly appears to be natural gas and behind-the-meter power, not just renewables; that has direct ERCOT relevance because it could increase proposals for on-site generation, islandable configurations, and hybrid load-resource models that complicate reliability oversight, market participation, and emissions policy debates (Business Insider; PRNewswire)
- Texas continues to attract energy-intensive digital infrastructure and industrial development, including Permian-integrated power/digital projects and a state grant supporting the Round Top rare earth project; together these reinforce longer-term ERCOT load growth in West and South Texas and strengthen the case for transmission expansion, resource adequacy planning, and local voltage support in emerging industrial corridors (GlobeNewswire; GlobeNewswire)
