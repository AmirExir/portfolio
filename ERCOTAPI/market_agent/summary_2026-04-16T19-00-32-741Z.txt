- Federal scrutiny of data center electricity use is rising: EIA plans a mandatory nationwide survey, which could improve load transparency for large new loads and sharpen benchmarking of Texas/ERCOT demand growth from hyperscale and AI facilities. Better data could feed ERCOT planning, interconnection screening, and policy debates over who pays for grid upgrades. (Wired)

- Texas remains a prime destination for data center growth, with multiple reports pointing to power availability and cheap energy as the core attraction. For ERCOT, that reinforces the main system challenge: converting speculative GW-scale load announcements into credible forecasts, transmission plans, and operational readiness without overbuilding for projects that may slip or cancel. (Business Insider; ComputerWeekly; Pew Research Center)

- New Era Energy & Digital said it secured additional funding for an Ector County, Texas data center campus. If this West Texas project advances, ERCOT implications are likely local transmission reinforcements, queue and study pressure for large load interconnection, and potential congestion or curtailment risks depending on whether service relies on grid supply, co-located generation, or staged energization. (GlobeNewswire)

- Nationally, utilities are lifting capital plans as AI-related power demand accelerates, while Oracle’s large fuel-cell procurement shows developers increasingly pursuing behind-the-meter or supplemental on-site power when grid interconnections are slow. For ERCOT, that signals more hybrid connection models and tougher policy questions around reliability obligations, market participation, and cost allocation for large loads using partial self-supply. (Fortune; The Register)

- Texas data center tax incentives are drawing heavier scrutiny, with reporting that the state is forgoing more than $1 billion annually in tax breaks and broader criticism that governments often underreport subsidy costs. The ERCOT relevance is governance and planning: incentives may speed load commitments faster than transmission and generation can be delivered, increasing pressure to align economic development policy with grid readiness and consumer cost impacts. (Grist; The Register)

- Broader AI backlash and project reshuffling suggest not all announced data center demand will materialize on schedule, as seen in commentary on delayed/canceled builds and Microsoft taking over capacity originally tied to OpenAI in Norway. For ERCOT, this is a reminder to discount headline load growth, track project-specific milestones, and avoid treating every announced campus as firm demand in reliability and transmission planning. (The New Republic; Financial Post)
