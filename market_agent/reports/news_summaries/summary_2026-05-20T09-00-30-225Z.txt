- **Standard Chartered sharpens efficiency push:** The bank plans to **cut 15%+ of corporate-function roles by 2030** while targeting stronger profitability and higher income per employee — a sign large banks remain focused on productivity over headcount growth.

- **Treasury yields near cycle highs are splitting investors:** **Long-dated U.S. yields** are hovering near levels last seen in 2007, creating a high-stakes choice: lock in attractive income now or wait and risk further bond-market losses.

- **Fed leadership takes on political symbolism:** **Kevin Warsh’s swearing-in at the White House** breaks recent tradition and underscores how politically charged Fed leadership and monetary policy have become.

- **Meta doubles down on AI — with jobs shifting, not disappearing outright:** The company is reportedly **moving 7,000 workers into AI roles** ahead of broader cuts, highlighting a broader corporate pattern: AI is reshaping org charts as much as it is reducing them.

- **Oil edges up on Iran tension:** Crude rose after Trump warned that the **“clock is ticking” on Iran**, but the bigger market concern is thinning supply buffers, which could amplify any future geopolitical shock.

- **Chip supply worries hit sentiment:** **Micron shares fell** as a **Samsung strike** raised concerns about memory-chip supply disruptions. Even when tighter supply can support pricing, markets often react first to uncertainty.

- **Musk loses in OpenAI case:** A federal trial **dismissed all of Elon Musk’s claims against OpenAI**, removing one legal overhang but doing little to cool the broader rivalry in the AI sector.

- **New York transit disruption eases:** A **tentative deal ended the Long Island Rail Road strike**, reducing near-term commuting and regional business disruption in the New York area.
