- ERCOT posted a new RTC+B follow-on filing, NPRR1290, to resolve remaining protocol gaps for Real-Time Co-optimization plus Batteries. This is an implementation-focused cleanup that should reduce ambiguity in battery dispatch, ancillary service treatment, and real-time market operations as RTC+B goes live. The broader implication is continued rule refinement around storage integration, signaling more operational detail still being locked down for ERCOT’s evolving real-time market design. (ERCOT NPRR1290)
- ERCOT also posted NPRR1311 materials correcting Real-Time Reliability Deployment Price Adders for Ancillary Services under load shed in RTC+B. In practical terms, this targets a pricing logic error during scarcity/emergency conditions, which matters for price formation, settlement accuracy, and emergency dispatch incentives. Reliability governance takeaway: ERCOT is still tightening edge-case pricing mechanics to make emergency operations and market signals align under the co-optimized battery framework. (ERCOT NPRR1311)
- New market-rule activity suggests ERCOT is refining cost recovery for reliability commitments: NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost Process sunset from 2027 to 2030, while NPRR1346 would double the transportation adder in the Fuel Oil Price from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal. Together, these changes would preserve and strengthen out-of-market fuel cost recognition for thermal reliability support, especially for units relying on higher-cost backup fuel logistics. (ERCOT NPRR1345; ERCOT NPRR1346)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a new Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under emergency support provisions. Even though one new TAC ballot file was not machine-readable, the filing title indicates ERCOT is formalizing how reliability-contracted resources are capped in offers, which could materially affect emergency procurement economics, mitigation treatment, and bidder behavior. (ERCOT NPRR1342)
- ERCOT introduced NPRR1343 to charge a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market. This points to a market-efficiency reform aimed at reducing low-value or congestion-distorting transmission bidding behavior, which could improve DAM outcomes, congestion revenue right alignment, and scheduling discipline. (ERCOT NPRR1343)
- Governance and document-control changes continue: NPRR1344, NPRR1305, NPRR1304, and NPRR1294 move several Other Binding Documents directly into Protocol sections, covering the Texas Market Test Plan, Counter-Party Credit Application Form, Resource Node identification, and demand response data specifications. These are not major operational redesigns, but they do tighten approval pathways and reduce ambiguity for testing, credit, modeling, and DR data governance. (ERCOT NPRR1344; ERCOT NPRR1305; ERCOT NPRR1304; ERCOT NPRR1294)
- Large load interconnection rules remain active: NPRR1308 defines Large Computational Load in Protocols and was approved effective Aug. 1, 2026, while NOGRR289 would give LCL cooling load up to 365 days after in-service to meet ride-through requirements. The net effect is a more formal ERCOT framework for data-center-type load, balancing faster energization with tighter eventual performance expectations to protect system stability. (ERCOT NPRR1308; ERCOT NOGRR289)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR288 to allow Energy Storage Resources to participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service through a related NPRR. This is a meaningful reliability-market expansion for batteries, opening another pathway for storage to provide controllable reserves and support scarcity operations, while requiring corresponding operating-guide changes to define how ESRs perform in reserve deployments. (ERCOT NOGRR288)
- Grid operations and planning rules are also shifting outside the market stack: NOGRR286 would add 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important marker as ERCOT prepares for higher-voltage transmission operation; NOGRR287 would require TO/TDSP coordination to minimize overlap of essential loads during load shed, improving rotating outage execution and critical-load protection. Together these filings point to ERCOT hardening both future transmission operations and emergency distribution-level implementation. (ERCOT NOGRR286; ERCOT NOGRR287)
- Recently posted approved items include NPRR1287 on outage-coordination terminology and process alignment, NPRR1293 clarifying modeling timeline milestones, and NPRR1298/NOGRR285 adjusting comment timing flexibility in the stakeholder process. These are incremental but relevant for planners and developers because they affect outage scheduling language, network model update timing, and how quickly market-rule decisions can move through ERCOT governance. (ERCOT NPRR1287; ERCOT NPRR1293; ERCOT NPRR1298; ERCOT NOGRR285)
