- Texas data-center growth remains the clearest ERCOT-relevant theme, with Applied Digital reporting stronger revenue on AI infrastructure demand and the Texas Tribune saying state tax incentives for data centers now exceed $1 billion annually. For ERCOT, that points to continued pressure from large load interconnection requests, faster load-growth forecasts, and more scrutiny on whether transmission and generation additions are keeping pace with hyperscale demand. (CNA; The Texas Tribune)

- Multiple reports tied new Texas AI and chip projects to potentially very large power needs, including Intel joining Elon Musk’s Texas-based Terafab chip initiative and Yale’s report that Google plans to use a gas-fired plant to serve a massive Texas data center. The grid impact is that more large customers may seek behind-the-meter or dedicated generation while still affecting ERCOT planning, fuel supply coordination, transmission topology, and local reliability in fast-growing load pockets. (TechCrunch; CNA; Yale Environment 360)

- The broader buildout around AI infrastructure is also expanding Texas electrical equipment and site-readiness needs, highlighted by Mission Critical Group’s acquisition of McKinney-based TxLa Systems to increase modular and switchgear capacity. Even when these announcements are commercial rather than regulatory, they signal accelerating demand for substation, switchgear, and distribution equipment that can become a gating factor for energization timelines, generator/load interconnections, and ERCOT-area project delivery. (Financial Post/GlobeNewswire)
