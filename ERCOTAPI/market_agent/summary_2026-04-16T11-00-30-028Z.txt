- New Era Energy & Digital said it secured additional funding for an Ector County data center campus in West Texas, reinforcing that Permian-area load growth is moving from concept to financeable development and could translate into sizable new ERCOT interconnection and delivery needs if the project advances on schedule. (GlobeNewswire, “New Era Energy & Digital Secures Funding for Development of its Ector County, Texas Data Center Campus”)  
- Multiple reports point to Texas as a prime destination for new AI/data-center development because of comparatively available power and land, but the common theme is that grid access is becoming the main bottleneck—implying more pressure on ERCOT transmission planning, large-load interconnection processes, and timelines for serving hyperscale demand. (Business Insider, “The Midwest's rise to America's second-biggest data center hub is fueled by these key states”; ComputerWeekly, “Go West! US datacentres head for available and cheap energy”)  
- The broader U.S. response to AI load growth is shifting toward on-site generation and very large utility capex, a signal relevant to ERCOT because Texas large loads may increasingly seek behind-the-meter gas, fuel cells, or hybrid supply options where grid service and turbine procurement lag; that could complicate load forecasting, resource adequacy assumptions, and interconnection studies. (Fortune, “U.S. utilities are planning a $1.4 trillion spending spree…”; The Register, “Oracle taps Bloom for 2.8 GW of fuel cells to keep datacenter binge going”)  
- Texas is reportedly providing more than $1 billion per year in data-center tax breaks, which strengthens the state’s competitive pull for power-intensive development but also raises a governance question for ERCOT stakeholders: whether economic incentives are outpacing the grid buildout, market design, and cost-allocation frameworks needed to reliably serve that demand. (Grist / Texas Tribune, “Texas is giving data centers more than $1 billion in tax breaks each year”)
