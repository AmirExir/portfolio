- **Markets shrug off geopolitics:** U.S. stock futures edged higher after the S&P 500 erased losses tied to the Iran conflict, signaling investors are still buying dips despite elevated Middle East risk.

- **Fed-rate caution remains the policy tone:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. should “wait and see” before cutting rates, reinforcing the view that geopolitics and inflation-sensitive energy markets are keeping policymakers on hold.

- **China trade momentum is softening:** China’s export growth slowed as Middle East turmoil weighed on trade, while higher fuel costs and stronger tech imports lifted imports—an early sign of pressure on global supply chains and margins.

- **Evergrande saga hits a symbolic endpoint:** Founder Xu Jiayin’s guilty plea to fraud and bribery charges underscores how China’s property bust has shifted from a financial crisis to a legal and political reckoning.

- **Wall Street earnings are strong, but not enough to calm nerves:** Goldman Sachs posted a record quarter in banking and trading, yet shares fell—suggesting investors care less about backward-looking results than about war, rates, and dealmaking risk.

- **Private credit tests market appetite again:** Blue Owl raised $400 million in the bond market, the first such deal in over a month, hinting that funding windows are reopening—but only cautiously amid concerns about underwriting standards and AI-related disruption.

- **Software and AI names rebound after a sharp reset:** Oracle led a strong bounce in software stocks after last week’s selloff tied to AI-competition fears, showing investors are reassessing whether the reaction was overdone.

- **Nvidia rumor jolts PC names:** Dell and HP shares jumped on acquisition speculation, even after Nvidia denied pursuing a transformative PC deal—evidence that AI-adjacent M&A narratives are moving stocks on thin evidence.

- **Crypto risk appetite is back:** Bitcoin surged about 6% toward $75,000 as short liquidations accelerated, reflecting a broader return of speculative positioning alongside steadier equity sentiment.

- **EV competition keeps intensifying:** Mercedes-Benz unveiled an upgraded EQS with longer range, faster 800V charging, and steer-by-wire, highlighting how premium automakers are still pushing tech differentiation to defend pricing power.

- **Nissan signals a longer-term reboot:** The automaker teased a new Xterra and outlined a turnaround centered on AI, hybrids, and product renewal—important for a company trying to rebuild relevance rather than simply cut costs.

- **Tech regulation and privacy remain a live business risk:** Civil-liberties groups warned Meta against adding facial recognition to smart glasses, a reminder that wearables innovation may increasingly run into legal and reputational resistance.
