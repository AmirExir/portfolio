- Texas local opposition to high-load sites is intensifying, with a lawsuit against MARA’s Granbury bitcoin mine over noise and health impacts and broader state-level concern about data center impacts on communities. For ERCOT, that raises siting, permitting, and political risk around flexible large loads even where interconnection is available. (Decrypt; Daily Signal)
- Riot’s expansion of AMD-contracted capacity at its Rockdale campus and IREN’s energization of the 1.4 GW Sweetwater 1 site show Texas remains a leading market for repurposing crypto-oriented campuses into AI/HPC load. ERCOT implications are higher large-load interconnection pressure, need for deliverability and curtailment strategies, and more scrutiny on how these loads participate during tight system conditions. (CoinDesk; Cointelegraph; GlobeNewswire)
- The common theme across multiple Texas-linked data center stories is that power availability is becoming the gating issue, not just land or capital. That points to stronger ERCOT demand growth risk, more requests for behind-the-meter or co-located generation, and greater importance of transmission upgrades and operational load flexibility. (Daily Signal; 24/7 Wall St.; OilPrice.com; Fortune)
- Social-license issues are becoming a practical grid-planning constraint: complaints about noise, lighting, and local disruption around mining and data center facilities suggest that even ERCOT-beneficial flexible load may face tougher county and legislative pushback. That could slow project timing and alter where new large loads can realistically connect. (Decrypt; Daily Mail; Daily Signal)
- Broader discussion around Texas transmission policy continues, with renewed arguments for more competitive transmission development as load growth accelerates. If that gains traction, it could affect how ERCOT addresses congestion, remote resource access, and infrastructure needed to serve new generation and large load clusters. (Watts Up With That?)
