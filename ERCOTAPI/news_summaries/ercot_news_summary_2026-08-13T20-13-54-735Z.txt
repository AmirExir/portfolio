- ERCOT posted PGRR145 creating a “Batch Zero” process for large load interconnections, a notable shift toward system-wide screening of data centers and other major loads before they advance. The immediate impact is tighter study gating and likely reprioritization of which projects move forward; the longer-term implication is more formal governance of large-load queue management and reliability risk allocation as load growth accelerates. Related ERCOT and stakeholder comment files were also posted, signaling active debate over implementation details. (PGRR145, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to give Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to comply with cooling load ride-through requirements when certain conditions apply. That suggests ERCOT is trying to balance urgent large-load energization with voltage/frequency ride-through reliability needs; planners should watch whether this becomes a transitional accommodation for fast-track data center connections or a broader precedent for staged compliance. (NOGRR289, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR288, paired with NPRR1340, to add Energy Storage Resources to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. This would expand battery participation in reliability commitments, improve flexibility in reserve procurement, and further integrate storage into real-time operational reliability tools rather than treating it mainly as an energy/arbitrage asset. (NOGRR288, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 to establish 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important signal that extra-high-voltage infrastructure is becoming more central to ERCOT operations. The rule change supports reliable operation of future 765-kV equipment and points to long-horizon transmission expansion needs tied to load growth, generator interconnection, and bulk power transfer capability. (NOGRR286, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 on load shed provisions to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. Operationally, this could improve under-frequency and emergency load shed execution by reducing inadvertent concentration on critical circuits; from a governance standpoint, it pushes TO/TDSP coordination and better mapping of essential versus interruptible demand, a key reliability lesson from prior emergency events. (NOGRR287, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to introduce a Day-Ahead Market bid fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids. If adopted, this would target behavior that clutters DAM with low-value transmission positions, potentially improving market efficiency and reducing uplift or distortive congestion trading signals; traders and congestion hedgers should expect closer scrutiny of bid quality and cost-to-submit economics. (NPRR1343, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a new mitigated offer cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT control area authority provisions. This matters because it affects how emergency/procured capacity can price into the market under mitigation, with direct implications for scarcity pricing, out-of-market reliability actions, and the balance between reliability procurement and market power mitigation. (NPRR1342, ERCOT)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19. While largely procedural, embedding the market test plan in the Protocols strengthens formal approval and change control, which should matter for implementation governance as ERCOT continues rolling out complex market design and systems changes. (NPRR1344, ERCOT)

- ERCOT planning and interconnection governance updates continue to tighten transparency and modeling standards: PGRR131 requires interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators, PGRR132 clarifies new Resources must interconnect through a new SGIA, and PGRR121 adds model quality and validation requirements for inverter-based storage. Together these changes point to a more standardized and data-driven generator interconnection process with stronger cost visibility and better dynamic model quality for reliability studies. (PGRR131/PGRR132/PGRR121, ERCOT)

- ERCOT also posted planning-rule refinements with operational implications: PGRR146 changes the schedule and naming of stability assessments for planned large generators and Large Loads, PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for P6/N-1-1 contingencies, and PGRR143 moves planning schedule postings to the public ERCOT website. Combined, these updates suggest ERCOT is adjusting study cadence and contingency-planning rules to handle faster project churn while improving process transparency. (PGRR146/PGRR147/PGRR143, ERCOT)

- A new ROS ballot spreadsheet was posted for PGRR146, though the file was not machine-readable in this feed. Based on the title, it likely captures stakeholder voting on the revised stability-assessment schedule for large generators and Large Loads, making it worth watching as an indicator of support for changing how often ERCOT reassesses dynamic reliability impacts from the interconnection pipeline. (146PGRR-10 ROS Ballot 080626, ERCOT)
