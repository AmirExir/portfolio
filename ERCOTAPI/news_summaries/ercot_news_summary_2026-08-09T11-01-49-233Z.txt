- ERCOT posted NPRR1340 and related NOGRR288 to allow Energy Storage Resources to participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, a notable step toward using batteries more directly for reliability deployments rather than limiting DRRS to traditional resource types. This could expand ancillary-service supply, improve fast-response flexibility, and reshape storage revenue stacking in ERCOT. A new ROS comments file was also posted, signaling active stakeholder debate on implementation details. (ERCOT NPRR1340 / NOGRR288)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to introduce a fee on inefficient hourly Point-to-Point Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market, targeting behavior that can add congestion or distort DAM outcomes without improving system efficiency. Market impact could include cleaner congestion hedging signals, lower uplift risk, and tighter DAM-SC pricing alignment if the fee meaningfully changes bidding behavior. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a new Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT’s emergency reliability authority. This is important because it addresses how emergency capacity contracts are priced and mitigated, affecting both out-of-market reliability procurement and market-power oversight when scarcity-driven support resources are brought in. (ERCOT NPRR1342)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, shifting testing requirements into the formal Protocol approval process. Governance impact is significant: market-system testing for major changes would become more standardized, more transparent, and less dependent on separate OBD handling. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- ERCOT posted PGRR145 establishing a “Batch Zero” process for Large Load interconnections, creating a transitional portfolio-style study for loads that meet maturity and commitment thresholds. This is one of the clearest signs ERCOT is formalizing how it will screen and sequence large emerging loads—especially data-center and computational demand—based on system-wide reliability impacts rather than purely serial project review. Stakeholder comment files were also posted, indicating material interest in queue design, study criteria, and fairness across competing large-load projects. (ERCOT PGRR145)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to give certain Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to comply with cooling load ride-through requirements. What happened: ERCOT is considering a phased compliance path for LCLs. Operational impact: this may ease near-term interconnection timelines for large computing facilities but could leave some new load less robust during disturbances until retrofits are complete. Planning implication: ERCOT is balancing rapid load growth accommodation against stricter disturbance-performance expectations. (ERCOT NOGRR289)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 to improve load-shed provisions so TOs and TDSPs identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. Reliability impact is straightforward: better feeder and critical-load coordination should reduce the chance that emergency curtailments inadvertently hit the same essential services repeatedly or shed the wrong mix of load during scarcity events. (ERCOT NOGRR287)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 to add 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important signal that ERCOT is preparing operating rules for extra-high-voltage infrastructure as the grid scales. Even without broader context, this points to long-horizon transmission expansion and a need to formalize voltage-control practices for future backbone facilities. (ERCOT NOGRR286)

- ERCOT planning and interconnection rule updates continue to tighten generator and load onboarding requirements: PGRR132 clarifies that new Resources must interconnect via a new SGIA, while PGRR131 requires interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators. Together, these changes improve cost transparency, queue discipline, and comparability of interconnection economics across projects. (ERCOT PGRR132 / PGRR131)

- ERCOT posted PGRR146 to move planned large generator and Large Load stability assessments from every three months to every four months, while renaming the assessment. The practical impact is a modest reduction in study cadence and administrative burden, but it also means planners will rely on slightly less frequent formal stability checkpoints as generator and large-load pipelines keep expanding. (ERCOT PGRR146)

- ERCOT posted PGRR147 to align allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for Category P6, N-1-1 contingencies. This matters for transmission planning because it clarifies what level of load interruption can be accepted under severe contingency analysis, influencing upgrade justification, compliance posture, and study assumptions. (ERCOT PGRR147)

- ERCOT posted OBDRR055 revising Non-Spinning Reserve deployment and recall procedures for RTC+B alignment. Operationally, this suggests ERCOT is continuing to tune reserve deployment rules so ancillary-service instructions better match real-time commitment and balancing processes, which can improve reserve usability and operator consistency during tightening conditions. (ERCOT OBDRR055)
