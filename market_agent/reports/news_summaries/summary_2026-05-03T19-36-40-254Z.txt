- **Berkshire starts the Abel era strong:** Berkshire Hathaway posted a sharp rise in operating earnings and built a record cash pile, signaling both business resilience and continued caution on big deals. The key watchpoint: whether Greg Abel deploys that cash through acquisitions.

- **Buffett spotlights Apple’s outsized contribution:** At Berkshire’s meeting, Warren Buffett publicly praised Tim Cook, underscoring how central Apple has been to Berkshire’s investment success. It’s a reminder that even Buffett’s “diversified” empire has benefited massively from a concentrated winner.

- **Spirit Airlines collapses after rescue fails:** Spirit is shutting down operations immediately after a proposed White House-backed rescue fell through. The broader takeaway: ultra-low-cost carriers remain highly exposed to financing stress, weak pricing power, and shifting travel demand.

- **Big consumer recall hits Thermos:** Thermos recalled **8.2 million** insulated containers over injury risks tied to pressure buildup. Beyond the safety issue, it’s another example of how product-design flaws can quickly become large-scale brand and liability problems.

- **UAE’s strategic shift away from oil dependence:** A Forbes analysis argues the UAE left OPEC because its vast sovereign wealth portfolio now matters more than crude prices. That suggests Abu Dhabi increasingly thinks like a global asset manager, not just an oil exporter.

- **Military AI adoption is accelerating:** Nvidia, Microsoft, and AWS are reportedly expanding classified AI work with the Pentagon. This reinforces that defense is becoming a major commercial channel for frontier AI, with implications for both revenue growth and regulation.

- **Energy shocks are hitting consumers fast:** Gas prices in Illinois jumped amid Middle East tensions and a refinery power disruption. The business angle: geopolitical risk and infrastructure fragility are still feeding directly into inflation-sensitive consumer costs.

- **Healthcare consolidation pressure grows:** Atrium Health is pursuing a combination with WakeMed, a move that could reshape North Carolina’s hospital market. Expect antitrust and pricing concerns, as scale in healthcare often brings both efficiency claims and affordability fears.

- **Texas hemp businesses get temporary relief:** A court order is allowing smokable hemp products to remain on shelves for now, pausing tougher rules and fees. It’s another sign that cannabis-adjacent markets remain heavily driven by legal uncertainty rather than pure consumer demand.

- **Fed and debt concerns remain in the background:** Commentary around Jerome Powell’s future role and the U.S. debt burden points to continued macro-policy uncertainty. Even without a single market-moving event today, fiscal strain and Fed succession are becoming bigger investor themes.
