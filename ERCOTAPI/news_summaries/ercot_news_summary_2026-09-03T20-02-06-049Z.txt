- ERCOT posted NPRR1347 to lock Energy Offer Curves for procured Contract for Capacity resources at the effective Value of Lost Load, tightening how scarce-capacity obligations translate into dispatch offers. That should make C4C resources behave more like reliability resources during tight conditions, with stronger scarcity pricing alignment and less discretion in emergency offer behavior. It also signals ERCOT is hard-coding reliability product design into Protocols as the capacity construct evolves. (ERCOT NPRR1347)

- ERCOT posted a cluster of fuel-cost and ancillary-service rule changes that could materially affect shortage operations: NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost process to 2030, NPRR1346 would raise the fuel-oil transport adder in the Fuel Oil Price, and OBDRR055 updates Non-Spin deployment/recall procedures for RTC+B. Together, these changes point to continued emphasis on operational flexibility and compensation for high-cost emergency-capable supply rather than a return to purely energy-only scarcity signals. (ERCOT NPRR1345, NPRR1346, OBDRR055)

- ERCOT is advancing large-load integration rules on multiple fronts: PGRR145 creates a “Batch Zero” process to study mature large-load interconnection requests system-wide, PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission requirements for large loads including large computational loads, and NOGRR289 would give some LCL cooling-load ride-through equipment up to 365 days after in-service for compliance. The near-term impact is more structured screening of data-center and other large-load additions, with better modeling but potentially more gated entry and transitional compliance obligations. Long term, this strengthens planning visibility and reliability oversight as ERCOT manages concentrated load growth. (ERCOT PGRR145, PGRR144, NOGRR289)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR288, tied to NPRR1340, to allow Energy Storage Resources to participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. That broadens the pool of fast, controllable reliability capacity available for real-time operations and could improve reserve deployment performance during ramps and contingencies. It also continues ERCOT’s shift toward integrating batteries deeper into core reliability products, not just energy arbitrage and regulation. (ERCOT NOGRR288)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 and NOGRR286, both with clear reliability implications: one would improve load-shed coordination to reduce overlap involving essential loads, and the other adds operational voltage limits for 765-kV equipment. The first could make emergency curtailments more targeted and socially resilient; the second is foundational for operating a higher-voltage backbone as transmission expansion scales up. Together they show ERCOT addressing both emergency response precision and future bulk-system operating standards. (ERCOT NOGRR287, NOGRR286)

- Market-governance and market-efficiency proposals were also updated: NPRR1343 would impose a fee on inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in DAM, likely aimed at reducing uneconomic congestion trading behavior, while NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Section 19 of the Protocols to standardize approvals. That mix suggests ERCOT is trying to tighten both trading incentives and governance formality as market complexity increases. (ERCOT NPRR1343, NPRR1344)

- Planning-rule updates continue to formalize ERCOT’s reliability framework: PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC expectations for Category P6/N-1-1 contingencies, while PGRR146 changes the cadence and naming of stability assessments for planned large generators and large loads. These are not headline-grabbing, but they matter for transmission planning assumptions, study workload, and the standards developers must meet to move projects through ERCOT’s queue. (ERCOT PGRR147, PGRR146)

- Several additional ERCOT pages were updated but appear to be navigational or library pages rather than substantive new filings, including Nodal Protocol Revision Requests, Current Protocols, Protocol Library, Workshops, NPRR Submission Process, Protocol Interpretation Request Submission Process, and Operations Messages. Their posting likely reflects linkage or publication workflow associated with the substantive revisions above, rather than standalone grid developments. (ERCOT market rules and committee pages)
