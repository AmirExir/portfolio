- Texas data-center growth is becoming the clearest ERCOT load-planning story: multiple reports highlight AI facilities’ fast-rising power and water demands, local opposition in Granbury, and broader concern that grid hookups and co-location are harder than developers expect. For ERCOT, that points to continued large-load interconnection pressure, transmission upgrade needs, and more scrutiny of realistic energization timelines. (Insurance Journal; Columbia.edu; Wood Mackenzie/GlobeNewswire)

- Community pushback to Texas data centers is now material enough to affect project execution, not just politics. Reporting from Granbury shows residents linking new facilities to noise, water, and infrastructure stress, which can slow siting, permitting, and ultimately ERCOT load forecasts if proposed projects do not convert on schedule. (Insurance Journal)

- New generation and storage additions continue, but at a scale far smaller than emerging AI-driven load requests. Terra-Gen said its 125 MW Lockhart III solar project reached commercial operations, while FPUSA/Bimergen announced conversion of 480 MWh of long-duration storage projects; together these reinforce ERCOT’s need for both new energy supply and flexible capacity, especially if large loads arrive unevenly by zone. (PRNewswire; GlobeNewswire)

- Wood Mackenzie’s warning that AI power strategies face technical and regulatory bottlenecks is highly relevant for ERCOT market participants: behind-the-meter, co-located, or fast-track power arrangements may not scale cleanly, increasing the value of queue discipline, transmission planning, and clearer rules for large-load service versus on-site generation. (Wood Mackenzie/GlobeNewswire)

- A broader national theme of slower-than-expected large-load hookups could provide short-term reliability relief if it also holds in Texas, but it cuts both ways for ERCOT: near-term reserve pressure may ease, while long-term planning risk rises if speculative load in forecasts does not match actual interconnection timing. (Financial Post/Bloomberg; Wood Mackenzie/GlobeNewswire)

- Texas remains a focal point for AI infrastructure and power-advisory activity, with Dallas/Fort Worth firms positioning around “unlocking power” for hyperscale projects. That underscores how transmission access, substation availability, and interconnection certainty are becoming central constraints in ERCOT development economics, not just land or tax incentives. (Associated Press press release)

- Consumer and investor interest in backup power and flexible infrastructure remains elevated years after Winter Storm Uri, reinforcing the persistence of resilience-driven demand in Texas. While small behind-the-meter systems do not solve ERCOT resource adequacy, they can modestly reshape retail load profiles and outage preparedness expectations. (Gizmodo)
