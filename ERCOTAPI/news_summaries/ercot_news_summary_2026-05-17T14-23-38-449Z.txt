- Texas AI and digital-infrastructure buildout remains the dominant ERCOT theme: Fermi said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity and is permitting roughly 11 GW of future Texas development, signaling continued pressure on ERCOT load growth, siting, and interconnection planning for very large power users and dedicated supply resources (Yahoo Entertainment)
- A Texas-focused digital-power developer, New Era Energy & Digital, highlighted strategic progress in the Permian Basin around integrated power assets and next-generation digital infrastructure, reinforcing that West Texas is becoming a key ERCOT battleground for co-located load, private generation, and transmission-dependent expansion (GlobeNewswire)
- Local backlash to large new loads is becoming a planning constraint in Texas: Hill County reportedly approved a one-year moratorium on new data center and energy storage projects, a sign that ERCOT growth may be slowed not just by grid constraints but by county-level permitting, land-use, and community acceptance risks (Insurance Journal)
- Supply-chain bottlenecks could delay ERCOT large-load energization and network upgrades: AI data center designs are said to require far more fiber, with cable lead times stretching toward a year, which matters for substation communications, telemetry, controls, and broader digital infrastructure needed to connect major new loads reliably (Tom's Hardware UK)
- National reporting on dedicated power plants for single data centers highlights an issue increasingly relevant for ERCOT: behind-the-meter or exclusive-use generation may accelerate load additions while raising policy questions around tax incentives, transmission cost allocation, backup service expectations, and whether those projects truly reduce or still indirectly burden the ERCOT system (Mother Jones)
- Broader U.S. backlash over data center power and water use suggests ERCOT planners should expect more scrutiny of large-load interconnection queues, resource adequacy impacts, and local reliability tradeoffs as Texas communities weigh economic-development benefits against infrastructure strain (Fortune, Forbes, The Verge)
