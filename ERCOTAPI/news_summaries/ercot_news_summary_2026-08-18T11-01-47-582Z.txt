- ERCOT posted NPRR1346 to raise the Fuel Oil Price transportation adder from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, a targeted fuel-cost parameter change that could increase offer flexibility for oil-backed emergency generation during tight conditions and scarcity events. (ERCOT NPRR1346)
- ERCOT filed NPRR1345 to extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost Process sunset from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030, signaling ERCOT still expects fuel-cost volatility to remain relevant to reliability commitments and generator cost recovery for several more years. (ERCOT NPRR1345)
- ERCOT opened NPRR1343 to add a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market, aiming to reduce low-value congestion trading behavior and improve DAM efficiency, congestion price formation, and hedging discipline. (ERCOT NPRR1343)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources, indicating continued refinement of out-of-market reliability procurement so emergency capacity can participate under clearer cost-mitigation rules without unduly distorting market outcomes. (ERCOT NPRR1342)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into the Protocols, a governance change that would formalize approval and change control for market testing and could tighten implementation discipline for future market-system releases. A related PRS ballot file was also posted but not fully readable, suggesting the item is actively advancing through stakeholder review. (ERCOT NPRR1344; 1344NPRR-05 PRS Ballot 081226)
- ERCOT continues advancing nodal settlement pricing for SODGs and SOTGs under NPRR917, replacing load-zone pricing with nodal energy pricing for these settlement-only resources; the practical impact is sharper locational price signals and more accurate settlement alignment with actual grid conditions. (ERCOT NPRR917)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to give Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet certain cooling load ride-through requirements, reflecting a pragmatic interconnection accommodation for fast-moving large load projects while preserving a compliance path for reliability performance standards. (ERCOT NOGRR289)
- ERCOT is refining large-load planning rules across multiple Planning Guide revisions: PGRR145 creates a “Batch Zero” process for system-wide study of mature large-load interconnection requests, and PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission requirements for large loads including LCLs. Together, these changes point to tighter screening, better load modeling, and more structured treatment of data center-scale demand growth in transmission planning and interconnection studies. (ERCOT PGRR145; ERCOT PGRR144)
- ERCOT posted additional large-load stakeholder material on PGRR145, including Skybox Datacenters comments, underscoring that the Batch Zero framework is drawing direct engagement from prospective large-load developers and could materially shape queue discipline, study prioritization, and network upgrade timing. (ERCOT 145PGRR-51 Skybox Datacenters Comments 042226)
- ERCOT continues reliability-rule work beyond large loads: NOGRR288 would enable Energy Storage Resource participation in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, NOGRR287 would improve load-shed coordination to minimize overlap with essential loads, and NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits—together indicating parallel efforts to expand flexible reliability tools, improve emergency operations, and prepare for higher-voltage grid expansion. (ERCOT NOGRR288; ERCOT NOGRR287; ERCOT NOGRR286)
- ERCOT also updated planning and interconnection transparency rules, including PGRR131 on mandatory interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators and PGRR146 changing the cadence of stability assessments for planned large generators and large loads from quarterly to every four months. These are process changes, but they matter because they affect project cost visibility, study workload, and the timing of grid-readiness decisions. (ERCOT PGRR131; ERCOT PGRR146)
