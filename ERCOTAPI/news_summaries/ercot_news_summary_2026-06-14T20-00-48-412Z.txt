- Texas data-center growth is becoming a direct ERCOT planning issue: multiple reports point to rising political and local opposition to large projects, which could slow some load additions even as developers continue pursuing powered sites in Texas. That creates a more uncertain large-load forecast for interconnection, transmission timing, and resource adequacy planning. (Business Insider; NBC News; GlobeNewswire)

- A new 100 MW Texas AI data-center project was announced on a 90-acre “powered land bank,” reinforcing that large flexible and high-density loads are still actively targeting ERCOT despite broader siting backlash. For ERCOT, that supports continued pressure on point-of-interconnection studies, local transmission upgrades, and questions around load ramp timing versus what is actually built. (GlobeNewswire)

- Texas is seeing more capital flow into retrofits and smaller-scale data-center conversions as an alternative to greenfield megaprojects, including an Irving colocation conversion financed with C-PACE. Operationally, that suggests some near-term load growth may arrive through existing industrial sites and distribution/transmission re-use rather than only through headline hyperscale campuses. (GlobeNewswire)

- National pushback against data centers is increasingly relevant to ERCOT because permitting, tax treatment, and efficiency requirements are emerging as policy levers; if similar constraints tighten in Texas, they could reshape the queue of expected large loads and reduce confidence in current demand-growth assumptions. (Business Insider; NBC News; Crypto Briefing)

- Wind-development friction outside ERCOT remains a watch item for Texas supply growth: lawsuits over delayed Pentagon reviews for onshore wind projects highlight a non-market bottleneck that could also affect future Texas renewable buildouts where military or radar reviews matter. Any slowdown would affect long-term energy mix, congestion patterns, and capacity expectations. (ABC News/AP)

- The start of commercial operations at the 3.65 GW SunZia Wind Project in New Mexico is not ERCOT-specific, but it underscores the scale of new remote renewable builds competing for equipment, labor, and transmission investment across the region. For ERCOT planners, that is relevant to interregional supply-chain constraints and the benchmark for utility-scale project execution. (EIA)

- Broader U.S. concern over AI-driven power demand and co-located private generation highlights a core ERCOT issue: whether large loads self-supply, seek grid service, or combine both will materially affect market revenues, transmission cost allocation, and reliability obligations. ERCOT’s challenge is not just megawatts requested, but how much coincident system dependence remains during stressed hours. (Crypto Briefing; OilPrice.com)
