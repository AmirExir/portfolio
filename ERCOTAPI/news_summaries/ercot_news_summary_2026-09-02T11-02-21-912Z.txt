- ERCOT posted NPRR1347 to clarify Contract for Capacity dispatch: procured C4C Resources’ Energy Offer Curves would be set at the effective Value of Lost Load, strengthening scarcity-linked dispatch signals and reducing ambiguity over how contracted capacity is offered in energy markets. This could materially affect pricing, commitment behavior, and performance incentives during tight conditions. (ERCOT NPRR1347)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1346 and NPRR1345, extending the Exceptional Fuel Cost process sunset from 2027 to 2030 and increasing the Fuel Oil Price transportation adder from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal. Together, the changes suggest ERCOT is preserving and updating out-of-market fuel cost recognition mechanisms for thermal reliability support, especially for oil-capable units during stressed operations. (ERCOT NPRR1346; ERCOT NPRR1345)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to add a Day-Ahead Market fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids, a market design move aimed at reducing uneconomic congestion trades and improving DAM efficiency. The likely impact is cleaner congestion pricing, less bid clutter, and tighter alignment between financial transmission positions and system conditions. (ERCOT NPRR1343)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, formalizing approval and change control. This is mainly a governance and market-implementation change, but it should improve transparency and consistency for testing major market system changes before go-live. (ERCOT NPRR1344)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 on Large Computational Load ride-through compliance, allowing certain LCL cooling loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet applicable ride-through requirements. That points to ERCOT balancing rapid large-load energization with grid performance standards, while signaling that data center interconnection flexibility will still be paired with eventual reliability compliance. (ERCOT NOGRR289)
- A new comment filing was posted for NOGRR289—“289NOGRR-05 Joint Commenters Comments 083126.” Even without full analysis of the DOCX, new stakeholder comments indicate active debate over how quickly large computational loads should meet cooling-load ride-through requirements, an issue with direct implications for fault performance, planning assumptions, and large-load onboarding policy. (ERCOT NOGRR289 comment filing)
- ERCOT posted PGRR145 and PGRR144, continuing to tighten the large-load interconnection framework. PGRR145 creates a “Batch Zero” process to study mature large-load interconnection requests system-wide, while PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission requirements for Large Loads including LCLs. Together, these changes are significant for data center load growth: ERCOT is moving toward more portfolio-level screening of load additions and more rigorous modeling of their transient and voltage impacts. (ERCOT PGRR145; ERCOT PGRR144)
- ERCOT posted PGRR146 to change the stability assessment cadence for planned large generators and Large Loads from every three months to every four months and rename the assessment. While administrative on its face, the change affects study workflow and queue management as ERCOT processes rising volumes of both supply- and demand-side interconnection activity. (ERCOT PGRR146)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 to add 765-kV operational voltage limits, an important operational step as ERCOT prepares for higher-voltage network expansion. This is a concrete transmission reliability signal: ERCOT is building the operating-rule foundation needed to manage future 765-kV equipment and associated bulk transfer capability. (ERCOT NOGRR286)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR288 and OBDRR055, both tied to operational modernization: NOGRR288 adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, while OBDRR055 updates Non-Spin deployment/recall procedures for RTC+B alignment. The combined implication is deeper battery integration into reliability services and more refined reserve deployment logic, which should improve flexibility in real-time operations. (ERCOT NOGRR288; ERCOT OBDRR055)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 to improve load shed provisions by helping TOs and TDSPs identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. This has direct emergency-operations value: better feeder/essential-load mapping can improve rotating outage execution and reduce unintended impacts to critical services during firm load shed events. (ERCOT NOGRR287)
- ERCOT posted PGRR132 and PGRR131, reinforcing interconnection process discipline for new generation. PGRR132 clarifies that new Resources must interconnect through a new Standard Generation Interconnection Agreement, while PGRR131 requires interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators. The practical effect is more standardized generator onboarding and better visibility into network upgrade cost trends. (ERCOT PGRR132; ERCOT PGRR131)
