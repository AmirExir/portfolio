- Texas’s data-center buildout is accelerating into a major ERCOT load-growth story, with reporting that the state is granting more than $1 billion per year in tax breaks and a new 540 MW “Project Caprock” campus breaking ground in Northwest Texas. For ERCOT, that points to sustained pressure on large-load interconnection studies, transmission expansion, and resource adequacy planning as hyperscale and AI demand keeps moving from concept to construction. (The Texas Tribune/Grist; GlobeNewswire)

- Multiple reports tied Texas AI infrastructure growth to rising natural gas and pipeline demand, including Permian-linked power deals and investor focus on gas-backed generation for new computing load. The practical ERCOT implication is that dispatchable supply, fuel deliverability, and local transmission constraints are becoming central to how the grid serves large new data-center clusters, especially in West Texas and other fast-growth zones. (Fortune; OilPrice; IBTimes Australia)

- The same AI/data-center boom is also showing signs of execution risk, with reports of nationwide project delays from supply-chain bottlenecks and growing community pushback over power costs, water use, and local impacts. For ERCOT stakeholders, that means some forecast load may slip in timing or location, complicating demand forecasting, interconnection queues, and long-range planning even as the overall trajectory for large-load growth in Texas remains strong. (TechRadar; Harvard Gazette; Digital Journal)
