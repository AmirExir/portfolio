- ERCOT posted NPRR1345 to extend the exceptional fuel cost process sunset from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030, signaling ERCOT and stakeholders still want a longer runway for generators to seek recovery of extraordinary fuel costs; this supports reliability during fuel-stressed periods but also preserves an uplift/cost-allocation issue in market design for several more years. (NPRR1345)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, which would formalize testing governance and standardize approval for market-system changes; that matters operationally because protocol-level control reduces ambiguity around readiness, implementation sequencing, and participant testing for major market updates. (NPRR1344)
- New unreaddable RMS ballot/comments files were added for NPRR1344, indicating the proposal is actively moving through stakeholder governance; even without full file extraction, RMS ballot and comment postings usually mark nearing decision points on implementation scope and participant obligations. (1344NPRR-03 RMS Ballot 081126; 1344NPRR-04 RMS Comments 081126)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to add a bid fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market, a targeted congestion-rights market design change that could discourage low-value DAM bidding behavior, improve market efficiency, and alter virtual/congestion trading economics. (NPRR1343)
- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 to create a mitigated offer cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT Control Area Authority provisions, suggesting ERCOT is refining how emergency or reliability-procured capacity is priced and mitigated; this has direct implications for scarcity-period procurement incentives and cost control. (NPRR1342)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR289 to allow up to 365 days after in-service for some Large Computational Loads to comply with cooling-load ride-through requirements, easing near-term interconnection timing for data-center-style loads while increasing focus on staged compliance and post-energization reliability oversight. (NOGRR289)
- ERCOT posted PGRR145 establishing a “Batch Zero” process for large load interconnections, a major planning response to the surge in large-load and data-center requests; it would shift ERCOT toward system-wide screening of mature projects before full queue progression, affecting transmission planning, study prioritization, and which loads are viewed as credible for reliability assessments. (PGRR145)
- Related large-load planning changes continue to build around the same theme: PGRR144 tightens dynamic model submission requirements for large loads including LCLs, and PGRR146 changes the assessment cadence for planned large generators and large loads from quarterly to every four months; together they show ERCOT is redesigning planning processes to better model fast-growing load interconnections without overloading study workflows. (PGRR144; PGRR146)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR288 to enable Energy Storage Resources to participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, expanding the operational toolkit for DRRS procurement and potentially improving ramping/flexibility during tight conditions while deepening storage’s role in reliability services. (NOGRR288)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 on load-shed provisions to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads, an operational reliability change aimed at improving under-frequency/firm load shed execution so outages better spare critical customers and reduce unintended over-shedding. (NOGRR287)
- ERCOT posted NOGRR286 adding 765-kV operational voltage limits, a notable signal that ERCOT is preparing operating-guide support for extra-high-voltage infrastructure expansion; this is important for future backbone transmission capability, voltage management, and long-range transfer expansion. (NOGRR286)
- ERCOT posted PGRR147 to align allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for Category P6, N-1-1 contingencies, reinforcing consistency between ERCOT planning criteria and reliability standards and potentially affecting how transmission projects and corrective plans are justified. (PGRR147)
- ERCOT also advanced several interconnection/governance transparency items: PGRR131 requires interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators, PGRR132 clarifies new resources must use a new SGIA, and PGRR143 moves a planning schedule posting from MIS to the public ERCOT website; together these changes point to tighter interconnection discipline and better transparency for developers. (PGRR131; PGRR132; PGRR143)
