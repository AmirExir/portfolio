- ERCOT posted a coordinated rule package to let Energy Storage Resources participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service through NPRR1340 and related NOGRR288; this would expand the pool of fast-response reliability capacity and better integrate batteries into ERCOT’s ancillary service stack. Planning implication: storage qualification, telemetry, and operating requirements will matter more as ERCOT leans on batteries for reserve adequacy; ERCOT also posted new staff comments on the item, signaling active rule refinement. (ERCOT NPRR1340 / NOGRR288 / 1340NPRR-10 ERCOT Comments 080426)

- ERCOT advanced a board-priority operating rule on Large Computational Load ride-through requirements via NOGRR282, setting frequency and voltage performance expectations for large data-center-style loads. Grid impact: stronger ride-through should reduce the risk that large blocks of load trip during disturbances and worsen frequency or voltage instability. Governance implication: new stakeholder comments from Cipher Digital and TIEC show the compliance design remains contested as ERCOT tightens large-load interconnection standards. (ERCOT NOGRR282 / 282NOGRR-34 Cipher Digital Comments 061126 / 282NOGRR-44 TIEC Comments 070826)

- ERCOT also posted NOGRR289, which would give certain LCL cooling loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet ride-through requirements. Operationally, that creates a transitional compliance path for rapidly connecting large loads, but it may defer some of the intended disturbance-performance benefits of the broader LCL rule set. (ERCOT NOGRR289)

- Large load interconnection reform continues with PGRR145, which creates a “Batch Zero” process to study qualified large loads on a system-wide basis. This is significant for planning because ERCOT is moving from ad hoc reviews toward portfolio-level screening of emerging hyperscale load growth, with likely implications for transmission upgrades, study queues, and energization timing. (ERCOT PGRR145)

- ERCOT posted PGRR144 to clarify dynamic model submission and review requirements for Large Loads, including Large Computational Loads. Reliability impact: better load models improve stability, fault, and voltage studies, which is critical as nontraditional loads become large enough to materially affect system behavior. (ERCOT PGRR144)

- ERCOT proposed PGRR146 to rename the Quarterly Stability Assessment and move planned large generator and large load stability assessments from every three months to every four months. Market/planning impact: this could modestly ease study cadence for developers, but it also changes how often ERCOT refreshes visibility into fast-moving interconnection portfolios; ERCOT posted new comments on the item this week. (ERCOT PGRR146 / 146PGRR-08 ERCOT Comments 080326)

- ERCOT filed NOGRR286 to add 765-kV operational voltage limits, a notable marker that operating rules are being prepared for extra-high-voltage facilities. Reliability implication: formal voltage guidance is foundational for safely integrating future backbone transmission and managing reactive power/performance at a higher voltage class than today’s core network. (ERCOT NOGRR286)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 to improve load-shed provisions and minimize overlap of essential loads during firm load shed events. Operationally, that points to more granular coordination between TOs and TDSPs so emergency curtailments can better preserve critical services while still achieving required MW reductions. (ERCOT NOGRR287)

- ERCOT proposed NPRR1343 to add a bid fee for inefficient hourly Point-to-Point Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market. Market impact: the change is aimed at discouraging low-value congestion hedging behavior, which could improve DAM efficiency and reduce administrative friction around transmission rights bidding. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- ERCOT proposed NPRR1342 to create a process for setting Mitigated Offer Caps for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT’s control area authority provisions. This has direct market-power and reliability implications because it affects how emergency or reliability-driven contracted capacity can price offers while under mitigation. (ERCOT NPRR1342)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into the Protocols. Governance impact: this raises the formal status of testing requirements and standardizes approvals, which should improve rule clarity for market changes and system implementations. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- ERCOT posted PGRR147 to align allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for Category P6, N-1-1 contingencies. Planning impact: this is a technical but important alignment that can affect how transmission projects and contingency performance are evaluated under reliability criteria. (ERCOT PGRR147)

- ERCOT updated NOGRR279 to extend deadlines tied to high-resolution recording equipment installation to January 1, 2029. Reliability implication: the extension likely reflects practical deployment challenges, but it also delays fuller disturbance-data visibility that supports event analysis and model validation. (ERCOT NOGRR279)
