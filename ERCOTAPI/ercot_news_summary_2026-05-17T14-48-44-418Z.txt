- Hill County approved a one-year moratorium on rural data center projects while it studies local impacts, highlighting rising Texas siting resistance to large new loads even as state officials question whether counties can legally block them. For ERCOT, the key issue is not immediate load loss but growing uncertainty around where hyperscale demand can actually interconnect and whether load growth shifts toward jurisdictions with fewer local barriers. Longer term, this points to more conflict between local land-use control and statewide reliability/economic-development priorities for large load integration. (Tom's Hardware UK; Insurance Journal)

- Multiple reports underscore that AI-driven data center growth is increasingly a Texas grid planning issue rather than just a tech story: developers are seeking dedicated power arrangements, behind-the-meter generation, and very large site pipelines. That raises ERCOT implications for load forecasting, interconnection queue management, transmission build timing, and cost allocation if large loads pursue bespoke generation or grid service structures instead of traditional utility supply. (Yahoo Entertainment on Fermi; Mother Jones)

- Fermi said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity and is advancing permitting for roughly 11 GW of future Texas AI campus development, signaling the scale of prospective large-load and supply co-development now targeting ERCOT. If credible, projects of that magnitude would materially affect zonal load expectations, congestion patterns, and the need for generation deliverability and backbone transmission planning in West Texas/Permian-linked areas. ERCOT planners should view this as a signal that AI load announcements are increasingly paired with dedicated supply strategies rather than pure retail interconnection requests. (Yahoo Entertainment)

- A reported Chevron effort to win a major Texas tax break for a power plant serving a single data center highlights a key ERCOT trend: large customers may prefer captive or contract-dedicated generation over relying solely on the shared grid. That could improve local adequacy for the host load, but it also raises governance and market design questions around resource participation, outage coordination, fuel assurance, transmission usage, and whether dedicated assets help or bypass broader system reliability needs. (Mother Jones)

- Supply-chain constraints are emerging as a practical limiter on ERCOT large-load execution: AI data centers reportedly require far more fiber infrastructure than standard facilities, and lead times are stretching toward a year. For ERCOT, this means some announced load may not materialize on the aggressive schedules assumed in market narratives, affecting the timing of actual peak demand growth, substation readiness, and transmission upgrade sequencing. (Tom's Hardware UK)

- Public and political backlash to data center growth is broadening beyond power prices into water use, land use, and perceived community impacts, which matters for ERCOT because non-electric constraints can delay or cancel projects that otherwise appear viable in load forecasts. The planning takeaway is that announced megawatt demand should be discounted for permitting/social acceptance risk, especially in rural Texas areas facing rapid AI-related development pressure. (Fortune; Forbes; The Verge)
