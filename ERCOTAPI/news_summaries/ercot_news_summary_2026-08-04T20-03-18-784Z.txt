- ERCOT posted a market-rule package to let Energy Storage Resources participate in Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service through NPRR1340 and related NOGRR288, expanding batteries’ role in reliability reserve deployment rather than limiting DRRS to traditional resources. This could improve scarcity response flexibility and ancillary service competition, especially during tight net-load ramps. Longer term, it signals ERCOT is continuing to redesign products and operating rules around storage as a core reliability asset rather than a niche participant. (ERCOT NPRR1340; NOGRR288)

- ERCOT posted PGRR145 creating a “Batch Zero” process for Large Load interconnections, allowing ERCOT to study qualifying large-load projects on a system-wide basis rather than only case by case. This is a direct response to the scale and clustering risk from data centers, crypto, and other large new demand seeking entry to the grid. Planning-wise, Batch Zero could become a major gatekeeper for transmission upgrades, deliverability expectations, and queue discipline for large loads in constrained areas. (ERCOT PGRR145)

- ERCOT also updated large-load planning rules with PGRR144 and NOGRR289: PGRR144 tightens dynamic model submission requirements for Large Loads, including Large Computational Loads, while NOGRR289 would give some LCLs up to 365 days after in-service to meet cooling load ride-through requirements under certain conditions. Together, ERCOT is trying to get better load behavior models while managing the operational reality of fast-moving load additions. The implication is stricter study visibility up front, but potentially phased compliance in operations where load characteristics are still being hardened. (ERCOT PGRR144; NOGRR289)

- ERCOT posted NOGRR287 on load-shed provisions to better identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. Operationally, this suggests more structured coordination between TOs and TDSPs to reduce unintended impacts during firm load shed events. From a reliability and governance standpoint, it reflects continued post-February-2021 refinement of emergency load-shed execution and critical-load mapping. (ERCOT NOGRR287)

- ERCOT’s 765-kV framework is advancing: NOGRR286 defining operational voltage limits for 765-kV equipment remains in play, and a new TAC report dated July 29, 2026 shows TAC recommended approval. This is a meaningful transmission-planning marker because 765-kV rules are foundational for operating any future extra-high-voltage backbone safely and consistently. If adopted, it strengthens governance and operating readiness for major bulk-system expansion. (ERCOT NOGRR286; 286NOGRR-13 TAC Report 072926)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1343 to add a fee for inefficient hourly Point-to-Point Obligation bids in the Day-Ahead Market. That points to concern about DAM inefficiencies or bidding patterns that may distort congestion hedging and market outcomes. If implemented, it could sharpen bidding discipline, reduce unproductive PTP volume, and improve DAM price formation and congestion signal quality. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1342 establishing a new process for Mitigated Offer Caps for Contract for Capacity Resources procured under ERCOT’s emergency authority provisions. This is a targeted market-power and cost-control issue tied to out-of-market reliability procurement. The planning implication is that as ERCOT leans on backstop capacity constructs, governance around offer caps and compensation will become more consequential for both reliability and market confidence. (ERCOT NPRR1342)

- ERCOT posted NPRR1344 to move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, standardizing its approval path. While administrative on its face, it gives market testing requirements a firmer governance foundation inside the Protocols. That should improve change control and participant clarity as ERCOT continues implementing complex market and system changes. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- ERCOT planning-rule updates continue to harden interconnection transparency and standards: PGRR131 adds required reporting of transmission-connected generator interconnection costs, and PGRR132 clarifies that new Resources must interconnect through a new Standard Generation Interconnection Agreement. These are not headline reliability changes, but they matter for queue transparency, upgrade cost accountability, and cleaner execution of generator entry into the system. (ERCOT PGRR131; PGRR132)

- ERCOT posted PGRR146 to shift the stability assessment cadence for planned large generators and Large Loads from every three months to every four months, and PGRR147 to align allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC expectations for Category P6, N-1-1 contingencies. Together, these updates affect how often ERCOT refreshes stability views and how it evaluates planning reliability criteria. The practical takeaway is a planning process that is trying to scale to higher project volume while staying aligned with bulk-system reliability standards. (ERCOT PGRR146; PGRR147)
