- Multiple reports point to accelerating AI and data-center buildout in Texas, with new financing for a Texas data-center project, strong results from Dallas-based Applied Digital, and broad coverage of rising power demand from AI infrastructure. For ERCOT, the key takeaway is continued growth in large-load interconnection requests, higher coincident peak risk, and more pressure on transmission, local delivery constraints, and resource adequacy planning. (Yahoo Entertainment; CNA; Internazionale.it)

- Texas’s policy environment is helping drive that load growth: The Texas Tribune reports the state is providing more than $1 billion per year in tax breaks for data centers, while other coverage highlights Texas as a preferred site for major AI and semiconductor projects. That combination could speed project announcements ahead of fully aligned grid upgrades, making ERCOT and TSP coordination on siting, load forecasting, and network reinforcement increasingly important. (The Texas Tribune; TechCrunch; CNA)

- Several items suggest large new Texas loads may seek dedicated or on-site supply rather than relying solely on the grid, including Yale E360’s report on a planned gas-fired plant to serve a Google data center in Texas and broader investment interest in power assets tied to data-center demand. For ERCOT, that raises planning and governance questions around how behind-the-meter generation, self-supply arrangements, and hybrid grid/on-site configurations will affect reliability modeling, market participation, and transmission cost allocation. (Yale.edu; Financial Post)
