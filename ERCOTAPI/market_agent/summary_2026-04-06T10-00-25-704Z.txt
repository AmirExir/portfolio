- Microsoft is in exclusive talks with Chevron and Engine No. 1 on a West Texas AI campus paired with new gas-fired generation starting around 2.5 GW and potentially expanding to roughly 5 GW, making it one of the largest announced large-load projects in ERCOT. The project’s colocated design suggests a behind-the-meter or dedicated-supply approach rather than relying solely on the existing grid. (Fortune, Financial Post, OilPrice.com)

- For ERCOT, the deal highlights how hyperscale data center growth is driving new dispatchable generation additions in West Texas, which could reduce direct system load exposure if self-supplied but still affect gas infrastructure, local transmission, and interconnection queues. A project of this scale would have meaningful implications for reserve margins, congestion patterns, and nodal pricing depending on how much power is grid-connected versus islanded to the campus. (Fortune, Financial Post, OilPrice.com)

- From a planning and reliability perspective, the proposed campus underscores ERCOT’s growing need to evaluate very large load interconnections alongside co-located generation, especially in areas already facing transmission buildout and load-growth uncertainty. It also signals a broader market shift toward bespoke bilateral power arrangements for AI load, which could complicate long-term load forecasting and transmission planning if more large customers bypass traditional grid-served growth. (Fortune, Financial Post, OilPrice.com)
