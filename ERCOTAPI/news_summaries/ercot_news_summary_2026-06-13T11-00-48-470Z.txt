- Texas policymakers are pivoting from recruiting large data centers to scrutinizing who pays for the grid buildout: multiple reports say Gov. Greg Abbott wants new limits so data centers fund their own power needs, a potentially major signal for ERCOT interconnection policy, cost allocation, and treatment of large flexible loads such as AI campuses and crypto mines. (Business Insider; Financial Post; Crypto Briefing)

- The practical ERCOT impact of the data-center debate is less about one project and more about queue, wires, and procurement risk: even articles downplaying the “panic” acknowledge very large new loads are concentrating in specific areas, which can force transmission upgrades, local reliability studies, and tougher expectations for onsite generation, demand flexibility, or direct cost responsibility. (The Atlantic; NBC News)

- A rising political and local backlash against data centers nationally could spill into Texas siting and permitting even if ERCOT remains attractive for load growth; for planners, that means more uncertainty around which announced projects actually materialize, complicating load forecasts and long-range transmission planning. (NBC News; The Atlantic)

- New DFW-area data center investment continues to surface, with an Irving conversion project receiving $11.6 million in C-PACE financing; while small versus hyperscale announcements, it reinforces that North Texas remains an active load-growth pocket where cumulative interconnection demand can tighten local delivery planning and substation capacity needs. (GlobeNewswire)

- A national clean-energy analysis claims Texas would see the largest cost increase if new wind and solar development is restricted, underscoring a core ERCOT planning tension: rapid load growth is colliding with debates over resource mix, and limiting low-marginal-cost additions could raise energy and ancillary-service costs while increasing pressure on thermal additions and transmission expansion. (PRNewswire / CEBA)

- Reports on stalled or delayed mega data-center projects outside Texas are a caution for ERCOT forecasters: very large AI-load announcements can be economically and politically fragile, so ERCOT load scenarios should distinguish between speculative headline gigawatts and projects with secured land, transmission, and power supply. (Financial Post; Crypto Briefing; NBC News)

- Growing discussion of dedicated “behind-the-meter” or colocated power for AI campuses highlights a possible ERCOT market design issue: private generation tied to large loads may reduce some interconnection stress, but it can also complicate transmission cost recovery, operational visibility, fuel assurance, and reliability obligations during scarcity conditions. (OilPrice.com; The Daily Upside)

- Broader commentary on AI infrastructure points to non-power constraints—equipment supply chains, cooling water, and cyber exposure—that matter for ERCOT too: even where electric service is available, delays in optical components, construction labor, water access, or security requirements can change the timing and shape of realized large load additions. (Help Net Security; Crypto Briefing; Construction Dive; Yahoo Entertainment)
