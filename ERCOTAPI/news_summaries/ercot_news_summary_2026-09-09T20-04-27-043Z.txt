- ERCOT posted a new cluster of market-rule changes centered on reliability product design and scarcity pricing: NPRR1347 would set Energy Offer Curves for procured Contract for Capacity resources at the effective VOLL, tightening how capacity-backed resources are dispatched and paid during scarcity conditions. This is a material signal for resource bidding strategy and future C4C implementation. (ERCOT NPRR1347)

- ERCOT also moved to extend its Exceptional Fuel Cost framework through 2030 under NPRR1345, preserving a longer runway for generators to seek recovery of extraordinary fuel costs. That should reduce near-term fuel supply risk for thermal units, but it also keeps uplift/cost-allocation and market-governance questions alive for several more years. (ERCOT NPRR1345)

- NPRR1346 would raise the transportation-cost adder in ERCOT’s Fuel Oil Price from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, improving alignment with actual oil logistics costs. The practical effect is better compensation assumptions for oil-backed emergency generation, which matters most in tight winter or outage conditions when dual-fuel capability becomes reliability-critical. (ERCOT NPRR1346)

- ERCOT proposed a Day-Ahead Market efficiency reform in NPRR1343 by adding a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids. If adopted, that should discourage low-value congestion trades, improve DAM quality, and reduce noise in transmission hedging behavior rather than changing physical reliability directly. (ERCOT NPRR1343)

- Governance/process changes are also advancing: NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, formalizing approval and change control. That increases transparency and procedural rigor around market testing as ERCOT rolls out consequential market-design changes. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- Large-load interconnection reform remains a major planning theme. PGRR145 would create a “Batch Zero” process to study mature large-load projects on a system-wide basis, while PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission requirements for large loads including large computational loads. Together, these changes point to ERCOT hardening its screening of data-center and other large load additions before they create deliverability, voltage, or stability problems. (ERCOT PGRR145; ERCOT PGRR144)

- ERCOT posted another large-load reliability accommodation in NOGRR289, allowing some Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to comply with cooling-load ride-through requirements under specified conditions. That may ease project energization timelines, but it also highlights ERCOT’s balancing act between accelerating LCL connections and enforcing performance standards needed for disturbance resilience. (ERCOT NOGRR289)

- Storage participation in reliability services continues to expand: NOGRR288, tied to NPRR1340, adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. This should deepen ancillary service supply and improve fast-response reliability capability, while also requiring operators and market systems to adapt dispatch and performance expectations for ESRs. (ERCOT NOGRR288)

- Transmission and emergency operations updates remain notable. NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits, a key prerequisite for reliably operating extra-high-voltage facilities as ERCOT’s backbone expands, while NOGRR287 updates load-shed coordination to minimize overlap of essential loads, improving controlled-firm-load-shed execution during emergencies. (ERCOT NOGRR286; ERCOT NOGRR287)

- Planning and compliance rules were also refreshed: PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for Category P6/N-1-1 events, and PGRR146 shifts large generator and large load stability assessments from quarterly to every four months. These are not headline-grabbing changes, but they affect how often ERCOT and developers reassess system impacts and how reliability criteria are documented and enforced. (ERCOT PGRR147; ERCOT PGRR146)

- ERCOT also posted OBDRR055 updating the Non-Spinning Reserve deployment and recall procedure to align with RTC+B operations. Even without a fully detailed procedural readout, the title and summary indicate operational instructions are being synchronized with real-time co-optimization, which is important for how reserves are converted into energy and recalled under tight system conditions. (ERCOT OBDRR055)
