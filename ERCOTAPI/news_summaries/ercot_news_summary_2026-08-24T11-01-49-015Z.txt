- ERCOT posted a cluster of new market-rule revisions centered on fuel and reliability cost recovery: NPRR1346 would raise the Fuel Oil Price transportation adder from $0.05/gal to $0.10/gal, while NPRR1345 would extend the Exceptional Fuel Cost process sunset from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030. Near term, that supports thermal unit cost recovery and can improve incentives for fuel-backed availability during tight conditions; longer term, it signals ERCOT is keeping post-winter fuel assurance tools in place rather than letting them expire soon. (ERCOT NPRR1346; ERCOT NPRR1345)

- ERCOT also posted market design changes aimed at day-ahead efficiency and reliability procurement: NPRR1343 proposes a fee on inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in DAM, and NPRR1342 creates a new Mitigated Offer Cap process for Contract for Capacity resources procured under ERCOT emergency authority. That points to tighter DAM behavior controls and more explicit price governance for emergency reliability capacity, with implications for congestion hedging strategies, mitigation exposure, and scarcity-period procurement economics. (ERCOT NPRR1343; ERCOT NPRR1342)

- Governance and rulebook process changes continue: NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, standardizing approval and change control. This is not a direct operating change, but it strengthens market implementation governance by putting testing requirements on firmer procedural footing before future system and market releases. (ERCOT NPRR1344)

- Large load integration remains a major theme. NOGRR289 would give Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to comply with certain cooling load ride-through requirements, while PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission and review requirements for Large Loads, including LCLs. Together, ERCOT is balancing faster energization of hyperscale/computational demand with a push for better planning models and eventual performance compliance—important for voltage stability studies, disturbance behavior, and interconnection screening accuracy. (ERCOT NOGRR289; ERCOT PGRR144)

- ERCOT posted another step toward integrating storage more fully into reliability services: NOGRR288, related to NPRR1340, adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service. Operationally, this expands the pool of fast-response reliability capacity; strategically, it further shifts ERCOT ancillary-service design toward battery participation and away from thermal-only assumptions. (ERCOT NOGRR288)

- Transmission and system operations updates point to deeper grid hardening and higher-voltage system readiness. NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits, and NOGRR287 adds load shed provisions to identify and minimize overlap of essential loads. The first supports reliable operation as ERCOT expands extra-high-voltage infrastructure; the second improves emergency load shed execution and could reduce collateral impacts during scarcity or under-frequency events. (ERCOT NOGRR286; ERCOT NOGRR287)

- Several planning-rule postings sharpen interconnection transparency and study discipline. PGRR131 requires interconnection cost reporting for transmission-connected generators, PGRR132 clarifies that new resources must interconnect via a new SGIA, and PGRR146 changes the schedule for stability assessments of planned large generators and Large Loads from quarterly to every four months. Combined, these changes affect project development timelines, cost visibility, and study cadence for both supply and rapidly growing demand. (ERCOT PGRR131; ERCOT PGRR132; ERCOT PGRR146)

- ERCOT updated planning and reliability modeling requirements for inverter-based and contingency performance. PGRR121 establishes model quality testing and validation requirements for inverter-based ESRs, and PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC under Category P6 (N-1-1) contingencies. These are technical but significant for how ERCOT validates IBR performance and sets planning reliability expectations under severe but credible events. (ERCOT PGRR121; ERCOT PGRR147)

- ERCOT posted OBDRR055 updating the Non-Spinning Reserve deployment and recall procedure for RTC+B alignment. Even without full procedural detail in the summary, the title indicates ERCOT is refining how Non-Spin is actually deployed and recalled under its real-time co-optimization framework—an operationally meaningful change for ancillary-service execution, telemetry expectations, and reserve performance. (ERCOT OBDRR055)
