- Texas data center growth is becoming a more direct ERCOT planning issue as multiple reports highlight rising political resistance, local zoning pushback, and concerns over electric and water use; for ERCOT this points to growing uncertainty around large-load siting, interconnection timing, and whether new loads will be required to bring self-supply or more flexible operating arrangements. (Dallas Observer; Business Insider; TechRadar; Naturalnews.com)

- San Marcos’ reported move to ban data centers via zoning, alongside broader rural opposition in Texas, signals that transmission and load-growth forecasts may become less predictable at the local level even if statewide demand remains strong; ERCOT and TSP planning could see more shifting project queues, location changes, and cluster risk around the loads that do move forward. (TechRadar; Business Insider; Dallas Observer)

- Reports that Texas data centers are under-reporting water usage and that communities such as McGregor are debating industrial expansion reinforce that large-load development is now a multi-constraint issue, not just a MW issue; in ERCOT terms, projects with weak water, land-use, or community support may face execution risk that affects load forecast credibility and infrastructure build timing. (Naturalnews.com; Waco Tribune-Herald)

- The broader AI infrastructure buildout continues to accelerate, with Crusoe reportedly seeking a $3 billion raise and Microsoft committing major capital to AI deployment; for ERCOT, the important takeaway is sustained pressure for new high-density, time-sensitive load interconnections that can materially affect capacity, congestion, and local reliability studies. (SiliconANGLE News; Financial Post; CNBC)

- Several national pieces warn that AI data centers create volatile, concentrated power demand rather than just steady load growth; that matters for ERCOT because fast-changing large loads can complicate forecasting, ancillary service needs, outage coordination, and local system upgrade requirements, especially where multiple projects cluster on the same transmission footprint. (IEEE Spectrum; Al Jazeera English; Researchbuzz.me)

- Advanced nuclear is increasingly being framed as a dedicated power solution for AI and large industrial load, including Texas-linked development stories; while near-term ERCOT reliability still depends on existing generation and wires, these projects could shape future interconnection, market participation, and behind-the-meter/self-supplied large-load models if they advance in Texas. (Fortune; Yahoo Entertainment)

- Fermi’s announced partnership with Primoris for power plant construction in Texas is a concrete signal that new generation is being organized around emerging load growth; likely ERCOT significance is added interest in dispatchable supply near major load centers or data center corridors, with implications for interconnection queue activity and future resource adequacy. (Yahoo Entertainment)

- Extreme heat and grid stress stories from other U.S. regions are not ERCOT-specific, but they reinforce a key Texas takeaway: as AI and other large loads expand, ERCOT’s summer reliability margin will depend increasingly on flexible demand behavior, local deliverability, and the ability to manage coincident peaks rather than assuming all new load is passive and continuously served. (Al Jazeera English; Naturalnews.com; New York Post)
