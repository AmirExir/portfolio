- Texas data-center growth is emerging as the clearest ERCOT load-planning issue: reports cite weak compliance with state water-use reporting, while San Marcos approved zoning restrictions on new data centers—showing that future large-load additions may be constrained not just by interconnection capacity, but also by local water and siting limits. This matters for ERCOT load forecasting, TSP planning, and resource adequacy assumptions tied to AI demand. (Natural News; TechRadar)

- A proposed $25 billion Vantage AI data-center campus near Abilene would represent another major West Texas large-load concentration, likely increasing pressure on transmission, queue management, and timing of network upgrades if the project advances. The significance is less the headline capex than ERCOT’s need to assess deliverability, coincident peak impact, and whether associated generation or transmission support is bundled with the load. (Crypto Briefing)

- New behind-the-meter and co-located generation models for data centers continue to gain traction nationally, with examples ranging from Chevron-backed Texas data-center power arrangements to reports on gas plants built directly for data centers outside normal grid interconnection pathways. For ERCOT, that raises market design and reliability questions around visibility of load, outage coordination, fuel assurance, and whether “off-grid” projects still create operational dependencies on the bulk system. (Yahoo/finance syndication; Reuters via Times of India)

- Fermi’s selection of Primoris to build balance-of-plant for the first six SGT-800 turbines at its Project Matador campus in Amarillo points to tangible progress on gas-fired generation tied to digital infrastructure in Texas. If completed, projects like this could add dispatchable supply, but they also reinforce ERCOT’s need to track hybrid load-plus-generation campuses for interconnection studies, transmission impacts, and operational telemetry requirements. (PRNewswire)

- Texas data-center expansion is increasingly linked to water availability and cooling technology choices, not just megawatts. Coverage on low response rates to Texas water surveys and growing focus on water-efficient or alternative cooling/power designs suggests future ERCOT large-load requests may face more scrutiny from local governments and state agencies, affecting project timing and the credibility of aggressive load-growth forecasts. (Natural News; Reuters via CNA/Times of India)

- Broader U.S. trends in home batteries and virtual power plants are relevant to ERCOT even without a Texas-specific filing here: rising residential storage adoption could eventually support peak reduction, ancillary services, and resilience if market participation pathways expand. The grid value is highest if ERCOT and TDSP frameworks can turn distributed batteries into controllable capacity rather than purely customer-side bill management. (Slashdot/Ars Technica; Yale e360)

- National commentary continues to underscore that softer near-term reliability outlooks can quickly reverse if large computational loads materialize faster than expected. For ERCOT, the takeaway is that data-center timing risk cuts both ways: delayed energization can ease short-term reserve margins, but concentrated step-load additions can still create local reliability and transmission challenges before systemwide capacity metrics show stress. (POWER magazine)
