- **Intel leads markets higher:** Intel surged about **23% after strong Q1 earnings**, putting the stock on track for a record close and reinforcing the view that legacy chipmakers can still benefit from the AI spending boom.

- **Tech and AI remain the market’s center of gravity:** The **Nasdaq opened higher**, with investors focused on semis and AI names. Broader sentiment suggests earnings tied to AI infrastructure are still driving risk appetite.

- **Google doubles down on external AI bets:** Google is reportedly preparing to invest **up to $40 billion in Anthropic**, a striking move that shows hyperscalers are willing to back multiple AI winners—even direct rivals to their in-house models.

- **AI competition is becoming more global and more intense:** China’s **DeepSeek unveiled a new flagship open-source model**, while Washington is reportedly scrambling over cybersecurity risks tied to advanced AI systems. The AI race is now as much geopolitical as commercial.

- **Meta faces internal strain despite AI push:** Employees are bracing for **major job cuts**, underscoring the tension between aggressive AI investment and ongoing cost discipline across big tech.

- **Fed succession risk moved into focus:** The Justice Department dropped its criminal probe of **Fed Chair Jerome Powell**, likely easing the path for **Kevin Warsh**. That raises the odds markets start pricing in a more tangible leadership transition at the central bank.

- **Wall Street’s trading machine keeps minting money:** **Jane Street posted a record $39.6 billion in trading revenue**, highlighting how market volatility and electronic trading continue to favor elite quantitative firms over traditional banks.

- **Energy markets may tighten further:** JPMorgan says **oil prices still have room to rise**, citing supply disruptions and insufficient demand destruction—another signal that inflation-sensitive sectors may stay volatile.

- **IPO market shows life in selective pockets:** **X-Energy’s IPO jumped**, delivering gains to backers including Amazon and Ken Griffin, suggesting investor appetite is returning for high-conviction growth and energy-transition stories.

- **Travel and consumer distress remain a weak spot:** **Spirit Airlines’ future hangs on bondholder decisions and possible federal intervention**, a reminder that lower-end travel demand and overleveraged balance sheets remain vulnerable even as equity markets rally.
