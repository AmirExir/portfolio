- Texas data center growth is increasingly colliding with local siting and permitting resistance: Hill County approved a one-year moratorium on rural data center projects, highlighting a new non-ERCOT bottleneck for large loads even where bulk power may be available. If replicated, county-level pushback could slow load forecasts, substation planning, and transmission build timing in fast-growing areas south of DFW. (Tom's Hardware UK; Insurance Journal)

- Chevron is reportedly pursuing a major Texas tax break to build dedicated generation for a data center, underscoring the “bring your own power” model emerging around hyperscale load in ERCOT. The key grid implication is that large loads may increasingly seek co-located or behind-the-meter supply to bypass interconnection delays and reduce exposure to ERCOT scarcity pricing, while raising policy questions about cost allocation and subsidy treatment. (Grist; Mother Jones)

- NRG’s new CEO said the company is leaning into AI-driven power demand with dedicated supply solutions and “virtual power plants,” reinforcing two parallel ERCOT trends: bespoke power structures for hyperscalers and heavier use of distributed flexibility to manage affordability and peak risk. For ERCOT, that points to more focus on large-load service models, retail-DR aggregation, and whether flexible demand can offset some capacity pressure from data centers. (Fortune)

- Multiple Texas-focused AI infrastructure developers are signaling very large power ambitions: Fermi said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity and is advancing permitting for roughly 11 GW of future development, while New Era Energy & Digital highlighted integrated power-and-digital expansion in the Permian. Even if these figures are early-stage, they reinforce that ERCOT planners face growing uncertainty around how much announced large load will become real, where it will land, and how much dedicated generation will accompany it. (Yahoo Finance/Oilprice; GlobeNewswire)

- A reported 4,032-GPU Texas facility built by Jane Street adds to evidence that Texas remains a preferred location for high-density compute, not just conventional cloud campuses. For ERCOT, this matters because GPU-heavy sites can produce steep, concentrated load growth with significant cooling and power-quality needs, increasing pressure on local transmission, substation capacity, and large-load interconnection studies. (Crypto Briefing)

- Supply-chain constraints tied to AI buildout—notably fiber cable shortages and longer lead times—could indirectly affect ERCOT project schedules by slowing data center energization, telecom backbone buildout, and possibly some substation/control infrastructure dependencies. The planning implication is that load additions may remain lumpy and harder to time accurately, complicating ERCOT and TSP assumptions on when announced demand actually materializes. (Tom's Hardware UK)

- The broader Texas debate over whether data centers are straining power and water systems is becoming more politically salient, with growing scrutiny of incentives, local approvals, and infrastructure burdens. For ERCOT stakeholders, the practical takeaway is governance risk: large-load policy, siting rules, and cost-responsibility frameworks may become as important as pure generation adequacy in shaping how fast new demand can connect. (Andymasley.com; Wattsupwiththat.com; The Verge)
