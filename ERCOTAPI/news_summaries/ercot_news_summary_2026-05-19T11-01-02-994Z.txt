- Texas data center growth remains the dominant ERCOT-relevant theme, with multiple reports pointing to rising power demand, local siting pushback, and developers pairing load with dedicated generation or “bring your own power” models rather than relying solely on existing grid capacity; this reinforces ERCOT’s near-term focus on large load interconnection screening, transmission timing, and reliability risk from concentrated load additions (Fortune, Business Insider, CNBC)

- Hill County’s one-year moratorium on rural data center projects highlights a growing local permitting and political constraint on large new loads in Texas; even if state law limits county authority, the dispute signals that ERCOT load growth may become more uncertain geographically and slower to materialize than interconnection queues alone suggest (Tom’s Hardware UK, Slashdot)

- New merchant-style power-and-compute campus announcements in Texas continue to surface: Fermi said it has secured more than 2 GW of generation capacity with permitting progress tied to roughly 11 GW of future development, underscoring how AI-related projects are increasingly coupling generation, land, and large flexible load in one development strategy that could reshape ERCOT planning assumptions in West Texas and other growth zones (Yahoo Entertainment/OilPrice)

- NRG’s new CEO is explicitly positioning the company around AI-related “bring your own power” solutions and virtual power plants, a sign that retail/load-serving and generation firms see a market for behind-the-meter, co-located, or contract-backed supply arrangements that could reduce direct strain on ERCOT peak planning while creating new operational and market design questions (Fortune)

- New Era Energy & Digital highlighted integrated power and digital infrastructure development in the Permian Basin, adding to evidence that ERCOT’s oil-and-gas-adjacent regions are becoming a focal point for hybrid generation plus data infrastructure buildouts, with implications for transmission congestion, interconnection prioritization, and local reliability support needs (GlobeNewswire)

- Jane Street reportedly built a 4,032-GPU facility in Texas, another example of high-density compute load landing in-state; while not an ERCOT filing, it reinforces that actual energized large loads are beginning to follow the AI narrative, increasing the importance of ERCOT visibility into ramp rates, load factors, and demand flexibility from new data-center customers (Crypto Briefing)

- Broader supply-chain reports on AI infrastructure point to fiber shortages and specialized electrical architecture constraints, suggesting some announced Texas data center loads may face non-power bottlenecks; for ERCOT, that means the risk is not just underbuilding for load growth, but also overestimating the timing and coincidence of speculative demand additions (Tom’s Hardware UK, Digitimes)

- Public scrutiny of tax incentives for data center-related generation in Texas is increasing, as shown by reporting on Chevron seeking a school-district tax break for a power plant tied to data center demand; the grid significance is that subsidized co-located generation could accelerate off-grid or partially grid-dependent load growth while shifting governance debates toward who pays for supporting infrastructure and local system impacts (Grist)

- Several national stories framed high electricity costs as a limiting factor for AI expansion; for ERCOT, that cuts both ways: Texas remains attractive on cost and development speed, but sustained large-load growth without matching generation and wires could erode that advantage through higher congestion, scarcity pricing, and more difficult reserve management (CNBC, Fortune)
