- **Markets inch higher, but inflation is the key watchpoint:** U.S. stock futures rose ahead of April producer-price data, suggesting investors are still betting the inflation trend may improve—but sentiment remains fragile.

- **Food inflation is reaccelerating:** U.S. grocery prices rose **2.9% year over year in April**, the fastest pace in over a year. The takeaway: inflation pressure is broadening beyond just energy.

- **Oil shock is rippling through the economy:** Reports of physical oil shortages tied to Iran-related supply disruptions are pushing up fuel costs and feeding wider inflation fears, with knock-on effects across transport, consumer goods, and margins.

- **Supply-chain stress is showing up in unexpected places:** In Japan, snack makers are shifting to black-and-white packaging because war-related disruptions are hitting ink inputs—a reminder that commodity shocks can surface in odd but real operational costs.

- **JPMorgan is preparing a major investment-banking reshuffle:** The expected leadership changes signal deeper restructuring at the top of Wall Street’s biggest bank and could reflect a push to sharpen competitiveness in advisory and dealmaking.

- **Walmart is cutting or relocating about 1,000 corporate roles:** The move underscores ongoing pressure on big retailers to streamline tech and product teams even as consumer demand stays uneven.

- **Amazon is raising the convenience bar again:** Its rollout of **30-minute delivery** for groceries and essentials intensifies competition in last-mile retail and puts more pressure on rivals’ speed and logistics economics.

- **Fed politics are getting more complicated:** With inflation risks tied to war and energy, any expectation for quick rate cuts may be unrealistic—even if political pressure for easier policy builds.

- **AI legal and governance scrutiny is deepening:** Sam Altman’s court appearance and reporting on his investment ties keep attention on conflicts, control, and governance in the AI sector, not just product growth.

- **Big Tech’s ambition keeps expanding outward—literally:** Reported talks between Google and SpaceX on orbital data centers show how far the AI infrastructure race may go, though economics remain highly speculative.
