- A 125-MW Texas solar project reached commercial operation, adding incremental near-term energy supply to ERCOT and modestly improving local reserve margins where transmission can deliver it; the practical value will depend on congestion, curtailment risk, and coincidence with summer peaks rather than nameplate alone. (Terra-Gen Lockhart III Solar Project Reaches Commercial Operations, PRNewswire)

- Multiple national stories continue to center on AI/data-center expansion in Texas, reinforcing ERCOT’s core planning challenge: very large load requests are arriving faster than traditional transmission, generation, and interconnection timelines can accommodate, raising the importance of load forecasting, queue discipline, and deliverability screening. (Fortune; Poynter; Associated Press; Financial Post)

- Texas remains a focal point for new digital infrastructure, with reports highlighting activity in Dallas-Fort Worth, Lubbock, and additional Texas campuses; for ERCOT this points to continued pressure on substations, transmission import capability, voltage support, and the need to distinguish speculative load from financeable, construction-ready projects. (Associated Press; GlobeNewswire; Yahoo Entertainment)

- Slower-than-expected data-center hookups are being cited as near-term summer relief for U.S. grids; the ERCOT implication is that connection timing uncertainty can temporarily ease peak-demand risk, but it also makes planning more volatile because deferred large loads can reappear quickly once power, land, and network upgrades align. (Financial Post)

- Texas data-center and AI ecosystem buildout is increasingly being paired with merchant generation, storage, and behind-the-meter strategies, suggesting ERCOT may see more hybrid connection structures and operational complexity around co-located supply, outage coordination, and market participation rules. (Yahoo Entertainment; Forbes; Cointelegraph)

- Transmission competition remains an active policy flashpoint in neighboring regions, with FERC-related complaints arguing over competitive bidding; while not an ERCOT filing, the debate is relevant because Texas transmission expansion costs, project selection, and incumbent-versus-competitive build frameworks will stay central as ERCOT needs major network upgrades for load growth and generator interconnection. (ETCC Says Utilities Ask FERC to Prohibit Electricity Transmission Competition..., PRNewswire)

- Broader Texas population and industrial growth stories continue to support the structural load-growth backdrop in ERCOT, especially in fast-growing North Texas; even when not ERCOT-specific, these trends matter because they compound data-center and industrial demand on top of already strong weather-sensitive peak load growth. (New York Post)
