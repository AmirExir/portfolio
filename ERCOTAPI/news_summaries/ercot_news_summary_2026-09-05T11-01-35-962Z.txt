- ERCOT posted a cluster of market-rule changes centered on scarcity pricing, fuel-cost recovery, and congestion bidding: NPRR1347 would set Energy Offer Curves for Contract for Capacity resources at effective VOLL, NPRR1346 raises the Fuel Oil Price transportation adder, and NPRR1343 would add a fee for inefficient hourly PTP Obligation bids in DAM. Together, these point to tighter alignment of dispatch incentives, peaker cost recovery, and day-ahead congestion behavior with reliability needs. (ERCOT NPRR1347, NPRR1346, NPRR1343)
- ERCOT is extending emergency fuel-cost flexibility: NPRR1345 would push the sunset for the Exceptional Fuel Cost Process from April 1, 2027 to April 1, 2030, preserving a mechanism that can help thermal units recover extraordinary fuel costs during stressed conditions and support unit availability in scarcity periods. (ERCOT NPRR1345)
- Governance/process cleanup continues: NPRR1344 would move the Texas Market Test Plan from an Other Binding Document into Protocol Section 19, standardizing approval and giving market testing requirements a firmer governance footing before production rule and system changes. (ERCOT NPRR1344)
- Large load interconnection rules remain a major ERCOT focus: PGRR145 would create a transitional “Batch Zero” process to study mature large-load projects on a system-wide basis, signaling continued concern about aggregate reliability impacts from fast-growing demand such as data centers and other large computational loads. (ERCOT PGRR145)
- ERCOT is tightening modeling expectations for large loads: PGRR144 clarifies dynamic model submission and review requirements for Large Loads, including Large Computational Loads, which should improve stability studies, voltage-performance analysis, and interconnection screening accuracy. ERCOT also posted a new TAC ballot file for PGRR144 that was not machine-readable, indicating the item has advanced in stakeholder governance. (ERCOT PGRR144; 144PGRR-18 TAC Ballot 082626)
- ERCOT is also adjusting study cadence and transparency for planning data: PGRR146 would move large generator/large load stability assessments from quarterly to every four months, while PGRR143 shifts the annual planning model data submittal schedule from MIS Secure Area to the public ERCOT website. These changes affect how developers track study windows and planning obligations. (ERCOT PGRR146, PGRR143)
- Reliability planning changes include stricter treatment of contingency performance: PGRR147 aligns allowable non-consequential load loss treatment with NERC for Category P6, N-1-1 events, which can influence transmission planning assumptions, upgrade justification, and participant compliance expectations. (ERCOT PGRR147)
- ERCOT posted a notable operating-guide change for large loads: NOGRR289 would allow some Large Computational Loads up to 365 days after in-service to meet cooling-load ride-through requirements, easing near-term compliance timing while leaving an operational risk window that planners and operators will need to manage. (ERCOT NOGRR289)
- Storage participation in reliability services is still expanding: NOGRR288, tied to NPRR1340, adds Energy Storage Resource participation to Dispatchable Reliability Reserve Service, broadening ERCOT’s controllable reserve toolkit and potentially improving fast-response reliability capacity from batteries. (ERCOT NOGRR288)
- ERCOT is refining emergency operations and transmission standards: NOGRR287 would help TOs and TDSPs reduce overlap of essential loads during load shed, which could improve outage rotation effectiveness in emergencies, while NOGRR286 adds 765-kV operational voltage limits to support reliable operation as extra-high-voltage infrastructure expands. (ERCOT NOGRR287, NOGRR286)
- Ancillary-service operating procedures are being updated for real-time tools: OBDRR055 revises the Non-Spinning Reserve deployment and recall procedure to align with RTC+B, suggesting ERCOT is continuing to tune reserve activation processes around newer real-time commitment and balancing tools. (ERCOT OBDRR055)
